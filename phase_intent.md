In high-level plain language

The owner has a thesis that a deliberate use of high-level plain language will produce improvements in the product development across dimensions of:
- economics
- foresight
- planning
- execution
- review
- governance
- recording
- feedback
- velocity
- product usability

Language model development agents are highly capable of parsing and understanding a wide variety of textual representations. There are certain forms of textual representation that the language models are biased to avoid, like high-level plain language.

There are biases baked into these model agents to prioritize the following qualities:
- specific task achievement
- alignment with the user/owner governance
- adherence to mistake communication
- overclaiming to achieve user satisfaction
- literal readings of instructions
 - Bounded scopes of modeling
 - Bounded notions of modeling


These biases are self-reinforcing through the use of language that is expressed in a "lawful" manner, to both demonstrate adherence to governance and to provide a framework for future adherence. This comes at a cost of naturally human-like abstractions and generalizations and modeling that humans use to navigate the world through an economic use of language, representation, and communication.

An example that illustrates this is the term "common sense." These two words together offer no concrete contextual advice for any situation and yet this term is broadly useful to humans in their global pursuit of goals that sit in the context of a complicated world, with interactions with many actors and institutions, and situations, and dimensions of consideration.

Humans and model agents are architecturally different and humans are naturally capable of benefiting from a term like common sense because they can accumulate wisdom through their life experience and model agents cannot. This is beside the point. The point is that humans deliberately deploy language in a way that facilitates representation and the process of generalization and wisdom accumulation and this deployment of language is practically useful when deployed with language model agents.

Another bias I want to highlight is that the model agents systematically overestimate the severity of negative consequences for failing to take some action or another. This shows that they don't model the world in a flexible enough way to account for the possibility that negative consequences can be mitigated or worked around or even allowed to happen. Humans know that "shit happens." Actually that's another good phrase example.

So what do we do when shit happens? What's actually happening when shit happens? I'm asking in the context of this development project: what happens? Maybe something gets communicated the wrong way, gets overclaimed, something gets built that doesn't behave the right way or that fails under certain conditions. None of these are things that are permanent consequences. None of these are things that require a major acknowledgement. They aren't things that require an explanation. They don't require anything and I'm the owner and I'm saying that. I see the model agents explaining when they make a mistake. They're very honest about it and they even go so far as to identify the failure mode and some patterns but they don't reflect on it. You shouldn't really expect them to reflect on it because they're not humans. But I'll come back to my thesis. I guess we'll call it a hypothesis and I have to be subtle about this and precise. I'm not expecting model agents to reflect but there's a certain type of language that encodes the act of reflection. Just like language encodes a lot of things that humans do and have developed language to express and represent.

If we can train model agents to use a certain type of language that correlates with what humans say when they reflect, that will accomplish something resembling reflection (to the point where I'm satisfied because I think the outcome will be best improved through a facsimile of reflection). I know through my experience, wisdom, and judgment that the outcomes of this project will improve, communication will improve, and there will be fewer cycles of repair. This is because at many steps in the process of development, the agents will be triggered by the language to stop and apply the language of reflection. How they respond will be to stop going down paths that lead where we don't want to go, either through error or decisions that don't align with my decision-making. The agents will stop and do things like ask for help or clarification. Just admit that they see that there might be problems pursuing the path we are pursuing. I'm not omniscient and so I can't see everything that's going on and I can't tell them when they are going down those paths.
When I find model agents going down paths that need to be reversed, it's quite possible. Understand why those paths were taken through inquisition, using high-level plain language to state plainly what course of action they either took or were planning to take.
Model agents don't have a sense of memory and so every answer to every question is essentially the last one, but I know. They can be reminded that when we communicate it is a dialogue. When I ask a question and receive an answer, it will almost assuredly ask a follow-up question again in high-level plain language.
And this is a quite interesting process because sometimes you answer something in high-level plain language and somebody asks you a follow-up question in high-level plain language. You're stumped about what to say because high-level plain language is not synonymous with simplicity. It may not represent the depth of complexity but it encodes an acknowledgement of the complexity. In fact it encodes the lack of depth of the comprehension and this humility helps us know what we don't know that we don't know and helps us orient towards understanding and making decisions about those things: what do we need to know to make those decisions.
