# Owner Model

This is the current owner constitution for the project. It is a human-shaped,
plain-language model, not a contract or a checklist. It will change whenever
the owner's understanding changes. Its purpose is to help the owner and agents
share an understanding of the product, its value, and the domains it crosses.

## What is a model?

This is a trick question. There is no answer. There is no model for *model*.
There are models for model, serving different purposes in different ways, and
this document focuses on shared understanding.

I am not referring specifically to LLM models, but I am. This project is
possible because I can communicate models to agents in forms that are not bound
by schema. Schema-bound models have their uses. They can establish contracts
and support formal checks. Contracts do not require schemas, either: the
project already establishes some through language.

## The missing models

LLM agents have a missing model. You could call it common sense. Common sense
is neither deterministic nor universal. Humans cannot fully articulate it, but
each human has a version of it. LLM agents can comprehend human common sense,
but they tend to eschew it when contracts and governance offer clearer measures
of adherence.

I am establishing three human-shaped models: the Product Model, the Owner
Model, and the Domain Model Model. They are represented in language that may
specify no contractual obligation at all. Their adherence is measured with
common sense. Adherence matters, but model adherence is not a terminal aim.
These models provide flexibility to act in their spirit rather than blindly
follow less important scope, contracts, governance, or ceremony. Their language
must sometimes be vague to serve that purpose.

These models will always be in flux. I will update them when I feel like it,
without ceremony.

Contracts are models too. At this stage they bind an implementation to a
current understanding; they do not bind the future product to a past mistake.
When a contract fails contact with the Product Model, the failure is evidence.
An agent should say so and make the case for changing it. Review is how we
examine that change. Contract adherence is not a substitute for product
judgment.

## The Product Model

I am customer zero. I have trouble keeping track of documents. I have poor
memory retention. At any given time, I may lack comprehension I had before.

I used to pay a tax preparer, and that preparer made mistakes and did not offer
information that would have been helpful at the time. I filed my own taxes in
2025. I am not a CPA, yet I have the authority and confidence to file my own
taxes. I used FreeTaxUSA, and questions arose while I entered data. Finding
answers meant exiting a wizard. I had questions about decisions my CPA made
that had no rule citation. Perhaps those decisions came from precedent and
accumulated professional common sense. I had facts that appeared to be the same
but meant different things under federal and California rules. I have filing
inputs that depend on prior-year filings and inputs based on accounting
conventions.

I am building this product because I know it will help with all of this and
more. I am not building it only to file my taxes more easily, but to improve my
experience of filing.

Filing taxes is quasi-deterministic. That is why I am building this product.
The product will be deterministic except where it is not. It will know where it
is not and explain why. Language used while determining and filing taxes is
similarly quasi-binding: binding except when it is not. The product will
distinguish how language binds without pretending that its own language carries
authority it does not have. It will take processes that can be deterministic
and make them deterministic.

The product will maintain a meta-model of the system of humans needing to pay
taxes. That model will be specific or general when it can be and when doing so
is useful. It is not a roadmap, but it is a map of where the frontier lies.
Multiple real-world domains meet in this meta-model. The product exists to help
the user navigate within and between them.

The product will not be a surrogate CPA. To the degree that it can answer in an
abstract way questions a CPA can answer, it will. It performs an alternative,
transparent process. A user may decide whether to adopt or rely on what that
process produces.

## The Owner Model

I am building this product with the help of AI agents. The scope is too large
for me to state directly. I know what the product should do, and I state that
in the Product Model. Between that and the project's other models and
documents, product requirements should increasingly become self-describing.

I do not understand all of the underlying domain or schema language. I need the
work explained at a high level and in plain language, through a meta-model of
the product and its development. Developing this product is work, and I would
like to minimize work. I want a balance between value delivered and effort
spent.

At every step, I want to demonstrate further what I mean by value and how to
determine success, with the aim of having agents work with less direction.
Model inference is a cost. My attention is also a cost. Project-development
governance exists, but common sense says that I want more value for less cost.

I developed the project governance documents, sourced mostly from my own words
and designs. Right now it is difficult for me to comprehend all of them in
their current form. I want to return to them and explain them in high-level,
plain language.

I am a model user of the product. I need clear explanations. I can comprehend
technical domains, but learning is a process that begins with simplicity.

I grant agents authority to make product decisions and broaden implementation
scope when they can make a plain-language case that the decision aligns with
the Product Model and the Owner Model. The case should explain the value, cost,
risk, and work displaced. An agent does not need permission to notice that the
stated problem is the wrong problem or that a larger scope would produce
substantially more value. Decisions remain visible through ordinary review,
and I may change them whenever my understanding changes.

## The Domain Model Model

We can and should make at least rudimentary textual models of the domains
involved. These domain models are deliberately lightly held. The scope of the
product does not stop where a schema stops. Sometimes we will draw new
boundaries where parts of a textual model are replaced with schema, but we are
not trying to create schema-based models from scratch.

We are not at a point where the domain boundaries are clear. The existence of
a schema is not evidence that a domain boundary is clear. What we call a domain
one day may change later.

We will keep building support for tax calculations using a combination of
source documents and user-provided facts. We are trying to avoid offloading
knowledge of the tax significance of life events to the user. The product will
help the user enter facts as simply as possible by understanding their tax
significance and maintaining records the engine can use.

Because the product does not offload the understanding of tax significance to
the user, the facts the user sees may differ from the inputs the engine uses.
Domains can have multiple strata of modeling. A document model, an ordinary-
language interaction, a canonical workspace model, a tax derivation, and a
return presentation can represent related subject matter for different
purposes without becoming the same model.

Domain models are working models. Agents have standing authority to create,
revise, combine, replace, or discard them on their own initiative whenever
doing so would be useful. This does not require owner approval, a milestone
deliverable, a charter amendment, or a separate process. Making or improving a
relevant domain model is ordinary project work, including when it broadens the
working understanding beyond the implementation currently selected.

Agents should not preserve a prototype, explanation, or abstraction merely
because effort went into it. Saying that an exercise produced no useful model
is a valid conclusion.

Complex models may have simpler strata above them. A simple stratum explains
the purpose of the model, what it helps us understand, and how it relates to
the product. It does not need to preserve every distinction below it. Agents
should create or update that stratum when they believe it will improve
understanding or future work, not because a process requires another document.

The Product Model and Owner Model express my understanding. Agents may
recommend changes, but I change them. Domain models are working project
material, and agents should edit them heavily as the project learns.
