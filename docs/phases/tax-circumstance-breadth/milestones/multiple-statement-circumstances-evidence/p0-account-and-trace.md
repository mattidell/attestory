## The person's account

### Two personal-sale Forms 1099-K

Imagine a person receives two Forms 1099-K for 2025: one from a marketplace for a used bicycle, and one from a payment app for a used camera. The forms report gross payment amounts; they do not tell the application what each sale was, what the person paid for the item, whether the item was personal or held for profit, or which fees, shipping, tax and refunds belong to that sale. The person can answer those questions separately and may correct one answer later.

The application can read each statement's reported Box 1a and withholding, but the tax meaning of a personal item sale depends on the underlying sale and basis facts. A supported personal loss has a Schedule 1 entry; a gain, break-even, unanswered circumstance or other named limit does not become that loss entry. The 2025 Schedule 1 instructions explicitly cover multiple Forms 1099-K: if the entire amount reported on the forms was in error or for personal items sold at a loss, enter the **total** of Box 1(a) amounts at the top of Schedule 1. When only part of a form's amount is in error or relates to a personal loss, the instructions use a different calculation and reporting path. Therefore, the return-level total is conditional; it does not imply every statement has the same disposition.

### Two mortgage Forms 1098

Now imagine two mortgage-interest reports. Each Form 1098 reports lender-level amounts and a point-in-time Box 2 outstanding principal figure. The person knows which home and loan a report concerns, who owes and paid the interest, and what the loan proceeds paid for; those are not all established by the reported boxes. The application can read report facts and ask ordinary circumstance questions. Tax law determines whether interest is deductible and how the debt ceiling applies.

Two reports do not by themselves prove two distinct loans. A transfer of servicing, a lender acquisition, or duplicate/corrected reporting can create more than one document concerning one debt. Conversely, two genuine loans can be secured by one qualified home. Publication 936's ceiling and interest worksheet consider mortgage balances together, with per-mortgage average-balance calculations; the 2025 Schedule A instructions also say “one or more home mortgages.” A Box 2 snapshot is not evidence that a debt stayed under a limit throughout the year. The sources reviewed here do not settle identity resolution for duplicate or transferred reports, and this investigation does not compute an allocation.

## What the adopted code currently does

### Form 1099-K

The adopted content inspected is core-calculations `v48`, registry `v46`, release `demo.release.sli-worksheet-integration.2025.v46.json`, circumstance vocabulary `v1`, and the Form 1099-K circumstance bundle `v3` (README's delivered-usage section). The statement family and Box 1a/Box 4 facts are defined in `packages/content/tax/2025/f1099k.bundle.v3.json`; ordinary answers are in `f1099k-circumstances.bundle.json`.

`record_f1099k_statement_durably` records each report as a family member whose fact identity contains `payer`, `statement`, and `tax-year`. Answers, corrections and withdrawals are addressed using those same three keys: `record_f1099k_statement_answers_durably` forms each answer fact ID from the question type and selected statement keys. Thus recording itself accepts multiple statements, and answer facts are keyed separately. `prepare_f1099k_statement_review` can select a specific statement by keys; without keys, it refuses when more than one current statement exists.

The adopted subject rule `rule.f1099k-statement-loss-reason.v3.json` declares Box 1a as its subject and counts statements sharing `tax-year`. When the count exceeds one, the rule publishes `multiple-f1099k-out-of-scope`. The declared subject-dispatch path can produce a reason publication for each Box 1a subject, but that publication is the same multiple-statement stop; the rule's ordinary-answer decision chain does not calculate each sale account in that case. The one-statement route publishes five figures and a categorical reason, while the Schedule 1 return entry is a distinct return-level consumer. Inspection shows the route's declared stop, not what a two-statement live run or saved explanation would contain.

### Form 1098

The adopted mortgage calculation is core-calculations `v45` in registry `v43` (README), with the successor circumstance bundle v2 and the mortgage interest rule `rule.f1098-line8a.v2.json`; its statement-reason companion is `rule.f1098-residence-interest-reason.v2.json`. Mortgage answer findings use the same `payer`, `statement`, `tax-year` key set as the source statement. The recording module can address an answer using explicit keys, but `_require_current_statement` currently requires exactly one current Form 1098 Box 1 source overall. The review module has the same unique-statement requirement. So the recording API's key-shaped arguments do not make multiple-statement entry available on the adopted route.

The line 8a rule requires the family to be closed and has a `count > 1` guard that blocks with `MULTIPLE_F1098_OUT_OF_SCOPE`; the per-statement circumstance reason rule is subject-scoped to the Box 1 statement. Because line 8a stops before reading the ordinary circumstances when multiple Box 1 members exist, current content does not combine each statement's answer and interest or establish a multi-loan limit. The adopted rule now reads box 2 and compares it to a limit for its bounded single-statement case, but this is not evidence of year-average or cross-loan computation.

### Marshalling, dispatch and result identity

`marshal_live_run_context` in `packages/derivation/marshal.py` projects current findings into rule inputs and collected sources. Ordinary unkeyed symbols cannot preserve disagreeing multiple findings: marshalling leaves a disagreeing ambiguous binding unbound rather than selecting one. Collection retains `SourceFact` keys. `live.py` emits rows named by collected sources, rule subjects/joins, and `shared_key_count` declarations; a required symbol is not automatically emitted as a keyed row merely because its fact type has keys. `subject_dispatch.py` evaluates a declared subject rule once per collected subject and joins other collected rows only by matching shared identity-key names; missing identity fails closed.

The Form 1099-K reason rule's current subject identity is the Box 1a fact, and the mortgage reason rule's subject is the Box 1 interest fact. Their declared `subject` metadata suggests item-scoped dispatch, while the explicit count guards stop the adopted result. A question's fact keys alone do not prove the consumer receives the corresponding statement's value; the rule's join, dispatch and saved presentation need execution evidence.

### Where result identities go

For Form 1099-K, `fact_id_for` in each recorder constructs source and answer fact IDs from the `payer`/`statement`/`tax-year` tuple. In `packages/derivation/subject_dispatch.py`, the subject is each current Box 1a `SourceFact`; its structured keys are used to join answer and figure input rows. Each of the five figure rules and the reason rule publishes a derived finding whose symbol includes the source Box 1a fact ID, not whose finding shape embeds those keys. In `packages/derivation/runner.py`, `evaluate_subject_scoped_rule` receives the parallel `publication_subject_keys` from dispatch and calls `_append_live_source_from_finding(finding, keys=subject_keys, ...)`; this is the point where a subject result's keys re-enter same-run sources for downstream subject-rule joins. That mechanism is read, not executed here.

`rule.schedule1-top-entry.json` is return-level and has no per-statement subject. It collects Box 1a sources and statement-loss-reason sources and returns one scalar. Its current `count > 1` guard blocks `MULTIPLE_F1099K_OUT_OF_SCOPE` before collection. Because that code is outside the runner's recorded blocked-code set (`RECORD_CODES` in `packages/derivation/runner.py`), `_record_blocked` stores the disposition code as `DEPENDENCY_INVALID`. Behind that guard, the rule's value expression sums every Box 1a only when every statement-loss reason is `supported-loss`, and blocks `DEPENDENCY_ABSENT` on any other combination. Removing the count guard alone would not implement the 2025 instruction's qualifying-subset rule; that remains an open combination policy. `presentation_projection.py` builds `f1099kStatementExplanation` keyed by statement entity; `review_f1099k_statement_with_saved_presentation` selects the entry by the selected statement key, then `associate_saved_run` in `f1099k_statement_review.py` checks completed `run_id` and ActLog revision. The top Schedule 1 field itself remains return-level. No saved multi-statement projection was executed.

For Form 1098, `rule.f1098-residence-interest-reason.v2.json` is subject-scoped to each Box 1 interest finding and produces a derived reason; `rule.f1098-line8a.v2.json` is a return-level line amount with a family count guard. The reason's source identity is recoverable from the source Box 1 keys passed through subject dispatch, while Schedule A line 8a is one scalar. `presentation_projection.py` projects `mortgageInterestExplanation`; `review_f1098_statement_with_saved_presentation` validates its account's `statementKeys` and `reportedInterest.sourceFindingId` against the selected report before calling the saved run association. `f1098_statement_recording.py` records answers only. No dedicated current Form 1098 report-recording operation was found under `packages/tax/`; the existing lifecycle test `tests/test_f1098_mortgage_interest_lifecycle.py` constructs synthetic Box 1 report findings directly in its fixture. A stage 2 report-source case must label that source as manually supplied test material unless a separately established product entrypoint is identified.
