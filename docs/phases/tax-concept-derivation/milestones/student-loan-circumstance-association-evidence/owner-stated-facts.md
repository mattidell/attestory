## On facts of the matter and their routes

1. There is a **fact of the matter**. At one stage of computation, there is a
   fact of the matter for **total deductible student loan interest**.
2. It **can be arrived at multiple ways** — either through "this is a collection
   of eligible student loans and their terms", or through "here is Form 1098-E
   box 1".
3. **Loan eligibility status does not directly impact** total student loan
   interest deduction.
4. Filing taxes involves **moving numbers from one box to another**.
5. **Facts of the matter are what they are, and they aren't necessarily more than
   that.**

## On what the application may require of a person

6. Users **don't necessarily know** who their loan issuer is and whether it is
   eligible. **The application won't require them to know.** That would be
   offloading tax domain knowledge onto them.
7. If the user does want to know "is there anything I should know about my
   responsibility when I put 1098-E values on my return", the application
   **poses to the user another fact of the matter**: *all of your student loans
   must be eligible.*

## On exchanges that may legitimately end

8. **The exchange may end there** — after the application poses that fact.
9. The user may say "I have a student loan that isn't eligible". **The exchange
   may end there.**
10. The application may ask "does that student loan impact this particular
    1098-E form?", and the user may say yes. **The exchange may end there.**

## On other routes and what a form does not carry

11. The application will also have a menu/action option somewhere called **"fill
    out student loan data into this spreadsheet"**.
12. The 1098-E **may not enumerate** all the loans, the issuers, and what school
    institution the user used it to attend or not attend.
13. There are **a lot of ways through** the facts and the data.
