# A1, stage 3 — resting points, and saying "I cannot say"

Completes A1's remaining `done` fields for the groups
[stage 2](a1-stage2-what-is-posed.md) covered. Stage 1 worked these only for the
statutory-classification group.

Settles no wording finally — that is the owner's to approve — and no consequence,
which is A3's.

## Group 1 — things a person ordinarily knows about themselves

**Resting points.** Each question stands alone, so each is its own resting point.
Answering one does not oblige answering the next.

*What rest leaves known:* the answers given, and nothing inferred from those not
given.

**"I cannot say."** Rare but real: someone may not know whether another return claimed
them as a dependent. The expression is a direct one — *I don't know* — and it must read
as a legitimate answer rather than a skipped question.

*What it must not do:* be treated as a "no". Not knowing whether someone claimed you
is not the same as knowing nobody did.

## Group 2 — what a lender's statement consists of

**Resting points.** Per statement. A person may answer for one statement and not
another, and a statement left unanswered does not put the others in question.

*What rest leaves known:* what was said about the statements answered. Nothing about
the rest.

**"I cannot say."** The likeliest case in this group: someone may not know whether an
employer's assistance or a 529 withdrawal was applied to the costs these loans covered,
particularly years later.

*What it must not do:* be read as either answer. A1 records only that it must remain
expressible and distinguishable; what a consumer does about it is A3's, and A3's
distinction between *no adverse information* and *adverse information of unresolved
scope* is the one at risk of being collapsed here.

## Group 4 — the enrolment circumstances

**Resting points.** The owner's three, and each is a place the exchange may
legitimately end: after the condition is stated; after the person volunteers something
adverse; after they confirm it concerns a particular statement.

*What rest leaves known:* at the first, what was known before. At the second, an
ordinary fact about at least one loan. At the third, that fact attached to an
identified statement.

**"I cannot say."** Two distinct forms, and they are not the same:

- *I don't remember what I was enrolled in that term.* An inability about the
  circumstance itself.
- *I know I wasn't enrolled, but I can't tell you which of these loans it affects, or
  how much of this total that is.* The circumstance is known; its scope or amount is
  not.

The second is A3's blocked state, reached honestly. The first is not — it leaves the
product where it was. **Both must be expressible, and they must not share an
expression**, because they have opposite consequences: one leaves the return
computable and the other does not.

## What must never happen, across all groups

- A resting point presented as an abandoned task.
- "I cannot say" recorded as an answer of either polarity.
- A question re-asked that the person has already answered.
- Any group's unanswered question used to infer an answer in another.

## What A1 still leaves

The exact wording of every question and every resting point, which is the owner's to
approve. A5 selects which of these the bounded consumer depends on; A3 owns what each
state does to a figure.
