# Track 7 — linked-loan decision brief

Prepared 2026-09-28 after the owner clarified that this milestone must not select whole-loan or portion treatment for a mixed eligible/ineligible-period borrowing. It is a bounded representation and evidence brief; ADR 0076 Part 3 remains open.

## Bounded path; no tax-position selection

The milestone can advance without deciding how “solely” applies when one borrowing paid for both a period that meets the eligible-student condition and a period that does not. Preserve the accepted default-supported eligible-student value when no enumerated adverse schooling circumstance is supported. For a known mixed-period borrowing, keep the tax consequence unresolved; do not turn it into a favourable default, an adverse result, or a fractional interest calculation. Whole-loan disqualification and a portion-based treatment remain deferred alternatives. This brief adopts neither and refutes neither.

The next useful work is factual representation: let the person describe which borrowing they understand paid for which schooling situations, which borrowing(s) the entered statement reports, and when they cannot say. This follows A5 stage 1’s provisional distinction between a borrowing reference and statement coverage ([A5 stage 1](a5-stage1-borrowing-and-storage.md)) and A1’s direction to ask ordinary circumstances rather than a qualified-loan verdict ([A1 stage 2](a1-stage2-what-is-posed.md), Group 3). The answer may establish a user-reported reference or statement-coverage claim. It does not establish legal indebtedness identity or resolve the tax interpretation. A recorded borrowing reference, same lender/account/disbursement dates, or two financed periods is not by itself application proof that records represent one legal indebtedness. Do not ask the person whether the loan is “qualified,” whether all expenses are “qualified,” or for an unsupported tax verdict. Do not require detailed loan-account terms/origination or institutional eligibility proof.

**Illustrative, provisional factual clarification:** “From what you understand, are these records about the same borrowing or different borrowings, which schooling situations did each borrowing pay for, and which borrowing or borrowings does this interest statement cover? If you can’t tell, you can say ‘I can’t say.’” The answer records the person’s account, not the application’s legal conclusion. Where a known mixed-period use remains unresolved under Part 3, the affected borrowing and any statement amount depending on it remain unresolved; unrelated borrowings/statements remain isolated.

There is no owner approval gate for proceeding with this representation/evidence work. Part 3 stays open until a later owner disposition or supported tax-contract decision; no election, override, or qualified-loan answer is introduced here.

## What the sources establish, and their limit

For 2025, 26 U.S.C. §221(a) permits a deduction for interest paid on a “qualified education loan.” Section 221(d)(1) defines that as indebtedness incurred “solely” to pay qualified higher education expenses meeting its listed conditions; subparagraph (C) makes one condition that expenses be attributable to education during a period when the recipient was an eligible student. Section 221(d)(3) ties eligible-student status to §25A(b)(3), which is framed with respect to an academic period. The statute does not state that multiple periods are pooled into one status, nor does it prescribe interest allocation by qualifying period.

The 2025 official CFR text of §1.221-1 repeats the solely/period structure, gives a one-note/two-qualified-semester example, and gives a whole-loan result for a loan partly used for residence improvements. The latter is a known non-education use, not a semester with failed student status. Its payment-allocation rule distinguishes interest from principal; it does not allocate interest by use or academic period. The two-semester example has both periods qualifying and does not answer the mixed-status question.

The regulation’s sunset condition can be checked against the later enactment. EGTRRA §901 made the 2001 Act provisions stop applying to taxable years beginning after 2010. The American Taxpayer Relief Act of 2012, §101(a)(1)–(2), struck EGTRRA title IX and the 2010 Act provision that had extended it, effective for taxable years beginning after December 31, 2012. Thus for 2025 the sunset did not remain in force unchanged: §221 as amended in 2001 was made permanent, and §1.221-2’s conditional post-2010 application of the pre-2001 statute is not triggered. The official 2025 CFR still prints that old condition, while §1.221-1 itself states an applicability end date in 2010. I therefore rely on current §221 for the 2025 rule and do not claim the printed legacy regulation clauses independently settle every post-2012 regulatory-applicability question. This enactment resolves the sunset premise; it does not resolve whether a failed academic period makes a mixed-use borrowing fail “solely.” The whole-loan and portion-based mixed-period readings remain deferred alternatives; Example 6 supplies historical context but does not resolve this case. This background is sufficient for the present representation/evidence work and does not select a tax position.

IRS Publication 970 (2025) is useful filing guidance, not authority for a mixed-period treatment. It summarizes the “solely” loan test and student/period requirements but does not supply a mixed-status-period allocation rule.

## Paper path and evidence to retain

1. **Circumstance, borrowing account, and statement coverage:** retain the ordinary schooling description as a student + academic period + identified institution + programme situation. Separately retain the person’s account of which borrowing paid for each situation and which borrowing(s) the statement covers, with “I can’t say” available. These are asserted references/coverage claims, not proof of legal indebtedness identity. Matching lender/account/disbursement dates or spanning two periods does not independently establish that identity. Neither record should assert that expenses or loans are legally “qualified.” Do not key schooling status to a loan: one situation may be financed by several borrowings. Keep concurrent institution/programme situations distinct.
2. **Period conclusion:** determine the period-level eligible-student result from each relevant circumstance and the already selected A0 posture. Retain exact supporting circumstance IDs, their current dispositions, the conclusion’s student/period identity, and whether its basis is adverse or default-supported. Institutional eligibility, credential recognition, and the school’s half-time standard remain responsibility statements, not additional proof requirements.
3. **Borrowing-level tax consequence:** Part 3 remains unselected. Preserve all user-reported financing claims for the represented borrowing and their source identity; do not infer legal indebtedness identity by choosing or matching one row. Existing `_one_source` behavior selecting one equal-valued row is not evidence that a set-level rule accounts for every claim. For a known mixed-status borrowing, record the tax consequence as unresolved unless/until a supported interpretation is adopted. Missing status, duplicate equal status, conflicting status, and a claim with unresolved scope need explicit behavior; sorting or dropping a row cannot decide the tax question.
4. **Statement coverage and amount:** retain the exact statement identity (lender, statement, tax year), user-reported borrowing references/coverage claims, and any unresolved membership/scope. A statement is a report, not the borrowing. If the statement amount depends on the known mixed borrowing whose tax consequence remains unresolved, preserve that affected amount as unresolved; do not invent a reduction or fractional amount. Unrelated borrowings/statements remain isolated.
5. **Amount and saved explanation:** retain the statement’s reported amount, every borrowing-level result contributing to its reduction or unresolved result, dependency pins/dispositions, the applicable default or adverse basis, and the path to the saved explanation. Preserve the existing worksheet as a separate output. Track 6’s saved reader roles remain experimental; its demonstrated bare/ordinary/adverse/unresolved reading is not evidence that this linked-borrowing chain reaches saved output.

Current ADR 0076 Parts 1–2 specify scheduling and relationship validation but do not bind the reduction-to-status relationship or decide Part 3. The path above is a design obligation only. No production schooling/borrowing recording-and-recovery module exists; existing ordinary-contribution and nominee machinery may inform a later design but does not demonstrate this path.
