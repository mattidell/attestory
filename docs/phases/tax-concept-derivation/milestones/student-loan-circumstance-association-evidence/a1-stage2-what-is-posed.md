# A1, stage 2 — what is posed, by quality of grounds

Continues [stage 1](a1-stage1-requirement-or-conclusion.md), which settled that a
statutory classification cannot be asked and a use-of-proceeds condition can be posed.
This stage covers the remaining facts A0 records as answerable, **grouped by A0's
qualities of grounds** rather than one per fact — two facts of the same quality may
share a shape.

Settles no wording finally: the words are the owner's to approve. Settles no storage
(A5) and no consequence (A3).

## Group 1 — things a person ordinarily knows about themselves

*Facts:* being legally obligated to pay the interest; not being claimed as a
dependent; the filer-level exclusions; filing status.

*What is posed:* these are asked directly, as questions about the person's own
situation. "Are you legally obliged to repay this loan?" is answerable because a
person knows whose debt it is.

*Why answerable:* no institutional fact and no rule is involved. Someone who
co-signed, or whose parent borrowed, knows which they did.

*Occasion:* return-level, at the point the deduction is being worked out. Not
prompted by any particular statement.

*Never asked:* whether their situation "qualifies" them.

## Group 2 — what a lender's statement consists of

*Facts:* the five composition witnesses — no related-person interest, no
qualified-employer-plan interest, no non-qualified loan component, no employer
educational assistance, no qualified-tuition-programme earnings used.

*What is posed:* one question per statement, about that statement's own amount.
These are already asked today and already keyed per statement.

*Why answerable:* each concerns the person's own borrowing arrangements, not the
lender's classification of them. Whether a relative lent them the money is ordinary
knowledge.

*Occasion:* when a statement is entered. Per statement, because the answer can
differ between them.

*A caution carried from A0:* the operator reading these yields one Boolean, so a rule
cannot branch on a member's answer — but the answers are keyed by statement and each
finding is pinned, so which statement an answer was about is preserved in the record.
Nothing here needs recovering.

## Group 3 — the statutory classification

*Fact:* every loan the interest was paid on is a qualified education loan.

**Cannot be asked.** Stage 1's finding, unchanged. What is posed instead is the
chapeau's ordinary condition — that the borrowing was **solely** to pay for school —
as a condition and never as an assumption already adopted.

*Occasion:* when the person asks what they should know about their responsibility, or
volunteers something, or chooses to enter loan information. Not as unprompted
interrogation.

## Group 4 — the asymmetric fact

*Fact:* the student was an eligible student for the period the borrowing financed.

*What is decided:* its behaviour. Always present as a consideration, never gating,
acting only as a disqualifier.

*Resolved by the owner, 2026-09-21* — and not by choosing between posing it and
omitting it. Both of those treated the condition as a question. See
[circumstances and responsibilities](responsibilities-and-circumstances.md).

*What is asked:* the person describes where they studied, what they were enrolled in,
and their course load. Ordinary circumstances, normalised by the translation layer.

*What is not asked:* whether the institution was eligible, whether the programme led
to a recognised credential, whether the load met that institution's half-time
standard. Those are **represented as conditions the person is responsible for** —
inspectable, bound to the circumstance and treatment that made them applicable, and
consumed by no calculation.

*What this removes:* the doubt-without-recourse problem. The product is not raising a
question it cannot help settle; it is telling someone which conditions are theirs, and
why they apply.

## What is posed when a person supplies a portion

*Occasion:* only after an adverse circumstance has been recorded whose affected scope
or amount is unresolved — never otherwise.

*What is posed:* whichever of the two routes the person can take. Either that the
circumstance covers the whole of a statement's reported amount, or the affected
portions with enough identification to attribute an amount. **Only what the selected
route needs:** a whole-statement answer requires no loan identity and no amount, and
an identified-portion answer requires nothing about the portions not named.

*Why answerable, and where it is not:* someone usually knows whether a circumstance
covered all their borrowing. They may genuinely not know what part of a reported total
a given loan accounts for, and A3 records that the state then persists rather than
being resolved by a guess.

*Never asked:* for detail unrelated to determining the portion.
