# A1, stage 1 — posing a requirement versus asking for a conclusion

One stage of A1, on one fact: F5, whether every loan the interest was paid on is a
qualified education loan. Taken first because A1's fourth question hangs on it, and
because if the distinction fails, the question cannot be asked at all and the rest of
A1 changes.

Nothing here settles A1's other five facts, the storage of any answer (A5), or what
the application does with an answer (A3).

## The two things that could be meant

**Asking for a conclusion.** *"Were all of your student loans qualified education
loans?"* This asks the person to apply a five-part statutory test — incurred solely
to pay qualified higher education expenses, for the taxpayer or a spouse or a
dependent, for education furnished while the student was an eligible student, paid
within a reasonable period, and not a related-person or employer-plan loan. It is
exactly the question the milestone's boundaries forbid, and a person answering it
confidently is the worst case: the answer looks usable and is not.

**Posing a requirement.** *"This deduction assumes the interest was paid on money
borrowed only to pay for school."* This tells the person what the rule needs. It
asks nothing. The person is free to say nothing, to say something that contradicts
it, or to ask what counts.

## The distinction holds, under one condition

It holds, and the condition is the substance of this answer: **posing a requirement
must not create an affirmation.** If the application treats a person's silence — or
their continuing past the statement — as agreement that the requirement is met, then
it has asked for the conclusion by other means and obtained a worse answer than if
it had asked outright, because the person never spoke.

That is the general form of a boundary the owner set at the start of the previous
milestone: absence of contradictory information is not a user affirmation. Here it
becomes a design condition rather than a caution. A posed requirement leaves the
product's knowledge exactly where it was.

So the honest description of what posing achieves is narrow: it gives a person the
opportunity to volunteer something they know and we do not. It does not gather a
fact, and a design that counts on it gathering one has misunderstood it.

## What is actually posed, and what is never asked

Posed, in a person's own terms:

> This deduction is for interest on money borrowed to pay for school. It assumes
> that's what these loans were for.

Never asked, because the person has no reason to know and the milestone forbids it:

- whether their school was an eligible educational institution;
- whether their programme led to a recognised credential;
- whether a loan meets the definition of a qualified education loan;
- whether they were an eligible student.

## What a person may volunteer, and what it is

Ordinary facts, in their own words, none of which is a conclusion:

| What they might say | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| "One of these loans I used to pay off a credit card." | A fact about what borrowed money paid for |
| "This lender's statement covers two loans and only one was for school." | A fact about a statement's composition |
| "I don't know how they combined my loans." | An honest inability, and a legitimate resting point |
| "My parents borrowed one of them." | A fact about who borrowed, bearing on a different requirement |

Each is answerable from ordinary memory. None requires the person to know a rule.

## Resting points, and what each leaves us knowing

| Point | What the product knows afterwards |
| --- | --- |
| The requirement is posed; the person says nothing | Exactly what it knew before. No affirmation, no contradiction |
| The person volunteers something adverse | One ordinary fact, and that it concerns at least one loan |
| The person confirms it concerns a particular statement | The same fact, now attached to an identified statement |

Each is a place the exchange may legitimately stop. Stopping at the first is the
common case and is not a failure by anyone.

## What this stage establishes, and what it leaves

Established: the question can be asked, in the form of a posed requirement, without
asking for a tax conclusion — provided silence is not read as agreement.

Left open, deliberately: what the application does with an adverse volunteer (A3,
including whether it is an established contradiction); whether the same posing shape
works for A0's other testimonial facts, which have different grounds; and the exact
wording, which is the owner's to approve once the other facts are done.
