# A1, stage 1 — posing a requirement versus asking for a conclusion

One stage of A1, on one fact: F5, whether every loan the interest was paid on is a
qualified education loan. Taken first because A1's fourth question hangs on it, and
because if the distinction fails, the question cannot be asked at all and the rest of
A1 changes.

Nothing here settles A1's other five facts, the storage of any answer (A5), or what
the application does with an answer (A3).

## The two things that could be meant

**Asking for a conclusion.** *"Were all of your student loans qualified education
loans?"* This asks the person to apply a **six-constituent** statutory test, as our own
P1 record enumerates them: incurred solely to pay qualified higher education
expenses (chapeau); the person on whose behalf those expenses were incurred, as of
origination (A); within a reasonable period of time (B); eligible-student status
(C); not related-person indebtedness; and not qualified-employer-plan indebtedness
(both from the concluding sentence). It is
exactly the question the milestone's boundaries forbid, and a person answering it
confidently is the worst case: the answer looks usable and is not.

**Posing a requirement.** *"This deduction assumes the interest was paid on money
borrowed only to pay for school."* This tells the person what the rule needs. It
asks nothing. The person is free to say nothing, to say something that contradicts
it, or to ask what counts.

## The distinction holds, under two conditions — and F5 is still not what gets posed

**Condition one: posing must not create an affirmation downstream.** If the
application treats silence, or continuing past the statement, as agreement that the
requirement is met, it has asked for the conclusion by other means and obtained a
worse answer than asking outright, because the person never spoke. That is the
general form of the owner's boundary: absence of contradictory information is not a
user affirmation.

**Condition two: the posing must state a requirement, not announce that it holds.**
This is the one an earlier version of this document broke. Its candidate wording said
the deduction *"assumes that's what these loans were for"* — which is the product
asserting a conclusion about these particular loans, in the speech act itself. A
person told that, who then says nothing, has been informed the premise is already
adopted. No later restraint about storing a `yes` repairs it. The owner's own shape
avoids this because it uses **must**: *all of your student loans must be eligible*
states a condition. Replacing "must" with "assumes" is exactly how posing smuggles a
conclusion without asking a question.

**And what can honestly be posed is not F5.** F5 is *every loan the interest was
paid on is a qualified education loan* — the whole § 221(d)(1) classification. No
wording asks a person that without asking for the conclusion. What can be posed is
the chapeau's ordinary requirement: that the borrowing was **solely** to pay for
school. A person can speak to that from memory. Establishing F5 needs the rest of the
chain, which nobody has asked them and nobody should.

So the follow-through A1's question 4 demanded, taken honestly: **F5 cannot be
asked.** The poseable requirement is a use-of-proceeds condition, and a person's
silence after it bears on neither.

## What is actually posed, and what is never asked

Posed, in a person's own terms, as a condition rather than an assumption:

> This deduction is for interest on money that was borrowed **only** to pay for
> school. Each of these loans has to be that kind of borrowing.

"Only" is load-bearing and its omission is a real defect, not a nicety. § 221(d)(1)
requires indebtedness incurred **solely** to pay qualified higher education expenses,
and 26 CFR § 1.221-1(e)(4) Example 6 holds that a note used *in part* for school and
in part for something else is not a qualified education loan. Drop "only" and someone
who borrowed for school and a car has been told something their loan satisfies, so
they honestly volunteer nothing. Under-posing loses the fact as surely as over-asking
corrupts it.

Never asked, because the person has no reason to know and the milestone forbids it:

- whether their school was an eligible educational institution;
- whether their programme led to a recognised credential;
- whether a loan meets the definition of a qualified education loan;
- whether they were an eligible student.

## What a person may volunteer, and what it is

Ordinary facts, in their own words, none of which is a conclusion:

| What they might say | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| "One of these loans I used to pay off a credit card." | A fact about what borrowed money paid for |
| "This lender's statement covers two loans and only one was for school." | A fact about a statement's composition |
| "I don't know how they combined my loans." | An honest inability, and a legitimate resting point |
| "My parents borrowed one of them." | A fact about who borrowed, bearing on a different requirement |

Each is answerable from ordinary memory. None requires the person to know a rule.

## Resting points, and what each leaves us knowing

| Point | What the product knows afterwards |
| --- | --- |
| The requirement is posed; the person says nothing | Exactly what it knew before. No affirmation, no contradiction — which holds only because the wording states a condition rather than announcing that it is met |
| The person volunteers something adverse | One ordinary fact, and that it concerns at least one loan |
| The person confirms it concerns a particular statement | The same fact, now attached to an identified statement |

Each is a place the exchange may legitimately stop. Stopping at the first is the
common case and is not a failure by anyone.

## What this stage establishes, and what it leaves

Established:

- **F5 cannot be asked.** It is a statutory conclusion, and no wording obtains it
  from a person without asking for that conclusion. A1's question 4 required this
  consequence to be followed through even if it came out this way, and it did.
- A **use-of-proceeds requirement can be posed** — that the borrowing was solely to
  pay for school — as a condition, never as an assumption already adopted.
- Posing it **does not establish F5**, and silence after it bears on neither. What it
  does is make the requirement visible so a person can volunteer what they know.

Left open, deliberately: what the application does with an adverse volunteer (A3,
including whether it is an established contradiction); whether the same posing shape
works for A0's other testimonial facts, which have different grounds; and the exact
wording, which is the owner's to approve once the other facts are done.

**A warning for the actions that build on this.** Text that was posed and not
contradicted is not F5, and must never be read as though it were. The no-affirmation
condition does not protect against that mistake, because it governs silence about
whatever was posed — not the distance between what was posed and what the rule
requires.
