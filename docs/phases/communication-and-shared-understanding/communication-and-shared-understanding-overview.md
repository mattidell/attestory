# Communication and Shared Understanding

Opened 2026-09-17. The opening milestone is the
[Shared Inquiry Pilot](milestones/shared-inquiry-pilot.md).

## Why this phase exists

The project needs understanding to travel: from the owner to an agent, between
agents working on different parts of a problem, and from one milestone to the
next. Instructions and records carry much of the detail already. What travels
less reliably is why the work matters, what is still questionable, and when a
different framing would help.

This phase develops the means and habits for that conversation. An agent
should be able to notice a questionable premise, ask a relevant peer, explore
an alternative, and bring the useful understanding back into the work. The
owner should be able to enter that conversation without reconstructing every
technical step or serving as the courier between participants.

The owner's [initial note](../../../phase_intent.md) and
[Owner Model](../../../OWNER_MODEL.md#learning-through-language-and-agent-judgment)
are the sources for this direction. The note preserves the owner's language;
this overview is an agent-maintained working interpretation. Their differences
are invitations to discuss, rather than something to conceal through wording.

## The idea we are putting into practice

Language can carry a developing model of the project. Phrases such as
"common sense," "think it through," and "shit happens" draw on connected ideas
about relevance, proportion, uncertainty, and recovery. A useful abstraction
can help an agent choose a better next action without specifying every action
in advance.

We will treat agents as participants capable of judgment: able to imagine,
question, disagree, ask for help, and revise an understanding. This is a working
approach to collaboration. The owner's thesis is that articulating what is
learned and passing that language onward can improve later judgment within the
project. The phase will explore that thesis through use.

Here, **alignment** means developing a shared understanding of purpose and
exercising judgment in its service. An agent can improve alignment by showing
that the current task, specification, or owner's framing misses something.
Agreement and literal compliance alone do not tell us whether understanding
improved. A dissenting answer may be the most useful contribution.

## What needs to become easier

- **Asking and answering across assignments.** A question can reach a useful
  participant without requiring the owner to copy it into another prompt.
  Conversations can include peers and groups as well as delegated tasks.
- **Moving between levels of understanding.** An accessible account of a
  specification can explain its purpose and connect outward to other work;
  technical detail remains available for decisions that need it.
- **Keeping a conversation alive across interruptions.** A returning agent can
  find the question, current understanding, and significant disagreement without
  reading an entire transcript. The account remains open to correction.
- **Using governance with comprehension.** A readable model can explain the
  purposes and relationships behind the existing documents, with routes to
  their precise text when needed.
- **Carrying lessons into judgment.** Useful experiences can become working
  concepts, examples, and changes to how a problem is framed, without requiring
  everyone to reread retrospective histories.

Technical records keep their purpose. A short explanation helps someone
navigate them; it need not replace their precision or duplicate every detail.
Likewise, a question on a board is a conversation, not automatically a task,
specification, decision, or permanent project record.

## Judgment and execution in conversation

Another inquiry is whether we can usefully distinguish critical thinking from
more mechanical execution, and how communication helps agents move between
them. A starting distinction is carrying out an understood approach versus
examining whether that approach answers the right question. These activities
can overlap: tracing an implementation may expose the mistaken premise, and
an apparently routine task may require judgment. They are not permanent types
of agent or a ranking of valuable work.

The owner reports that the foreman once combined coordination with trusted
high-level advice. More recently, a separate advisor has supplied clearer
analysis and found problems that survived the foreman's builder/reviewer loop.
The owner now frequently carries repair prompts between them. This is a useful
starting observation, not evidence that one model or role is inherently better
at reasoning. Model differences, distance from authorship, accumulated context,
coordination workload, and expectations attached to a role are possible
contributors whose effects have not been separated.

Direct foreman–advisor correspondence is a central case for this phase. The
foreman can bring implementation context and continuity; the advisor can
question the framing and bring another reading. Either can correct the other.
They should be able to explore a question before it becomes a repair order,
without requiring the owner to relay every exchange or making the advisor a
new supervisor over the foreman. The owner can still enter the conversation
and judge an unresolved choice.

We want to understand what changes through that correspondence: does an agent
see a dependency it had missed, reconsider the purpose of a task, or establish
that the existing approach was sound? Self-criticism, confident prose, and
reports of compliance do not by themselves answer those questions. Nor does
agreement between participants. Direct dialogue may help, or it may cause
participants to share an unchecked premise. Independent examination remains a
different useful activity. These are questions to explore through work, not a
new performance rubric or a requirement to classify every action.

## How the phase will develop

We will use the approach while planning the work. Early conversations may
change the milestones themselves. The [roadmap](communication-and-shared-understanding-roadmap.md)
offers a starting sequence and leaves later selections open.

Start with a small message-board experiment. Repository text is the preferred
first candidate because people and agents can inspect and edit it. Test the
channel separately from the mechanism that notices a question and wakes a
participant. A2A is a candidate to investigate if communication across agent
hosts needs it; adopting a protocol is not an outcome in itself.

Then use the practice during one or two real technical milestones. A readable
governance model and a bounded study of lessons and costs can develop alongside
those trials where their questions are independent. New roles may emerge from
repeated needs, without being invented before the needs are visible.

The owner directs an exploratory workflow for this phase. Goals and completion
judgments can evolve with experience. We will not turn the phase into a style
compliance program or require a scorecard for comprehension. Short exchanges,
sketches, and working models are available when useful; they are not mandatory
documents for every task. Existing review and technical checks still serve
changes to the application. Changes to operating practice will be stated where
they are tried, rather than implied by an experiment's success.

## What would make this worth continuing

Look for useful moments: a peer question changes the plan before a mistaken
assumption is built; the owner can pursue a thought without a technical
translation detour; a returning agent understands why the work exists; a later
task uses an earlier lesson without copying its retrospective. Also notice
when a channel adds chatter, when a summary hides disagreement, or when plain
language sounds clear while the participants mean different things.

These are starting questions for judgment, not numerical acceptance metrics.
A separate economic study can examine token use where observations are
available and comparable. It should distinguish model cost from owner attention
and from the usefulness of the resulting work, and leave unknown costs unknown.

At a phase boundary, the owner and agents should be able to explain which
practices are worth keeping, where they help, and what remains awkward. The
decision to conclude, extend, or change direction will follow that experience.
