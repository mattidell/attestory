# P0: a refused head of household status does not set the worksheet ceilings

Of the scripts and JSON files this note names, only `trace.py` and `design.py`
are kept (the tests load them); the others and their raw output were
working artifacts and are not kept. The numbers below are their record.

Published core v102, registry v100, release v100. Synthetic demo returns
only: wages 50,000, withholding 4,500, the standard deduction, no qualified
dividends and no sale. Live runs go through `live_coordinate_run`. The
scratch successor is core v103, registry v101, release v101, loaded from the
v102 loader entry. That label is the mechanical successor of v102 for this
experiment. eic-separated-married is using v103, so the published number is
a foreman decision.

## Result

On v102, `tax.us.2025.rule.qdcg-worksheet.line-6` and
`tax.us.2025.rule.qdcg-worksheet.line-13` read filing status alone. After a
head of household or qualifying surviving spouse "no", and when that answer
is missing, lines 6 and 13 still publish that status's ceiling. Lines 7 and
14, the only rules that read those ceilings, stay blocked because taxable
income is absent, so the return's tax does not change. The 2025 instructions
assign each ceiling to a filing status and tell the filer to check only a
status that applies. Scratch successors read `tax.us.2025.hoh.tests-met` and
`tax.us.2025.qss.tests-met` the way standard-deduction worksheet line 3
already does. Yes keeps the published lookup. No refuses the line and names
the answer. An unanswered answer blocks by naming it. Single, married filing
jointly, and married filing separately read nothing new. The route is
content. The full-surface replay of the 775 recorded lists passes with no
unexpected difference and no number change.

**What the page shows.** On a refused or unanswered head of household
return, Form 1040 lines 12e, 15, and 16 carry
`tax.us.2025.input-question.hoh-tests-met`: "You chose head of household.
Answer yes only if all three of these apply." The question ends: "If any of
these does not apply, you cannot file as head of household: change your
filing status (if you were married, that is usually married filing jointly
or married filing separately)." The qualifying surviving spouse question
ends the same way for that status. The trace cuts each stored sentence at
500 characters, which is before "cannot file as", so `question_on_page` is
empty. The full page text contains the phrase: 20 times on a "no", 19 times
when the answer is missing, on both cores.

The trace's ceiling counter adds the comma form and the raw digits and
stores the sum under the comma key. A second live render of the refused
head of household page splits that sum (`page-digits.json`). On v102 the
comma forms 64,750 and 566,700 occur zero times. The raw values 64750 and
566700 occur once each, as the explanation-graph values of lines 6 and 13.
On the scratch successor both raw values are gone and the question phrase
still occurs 20 times. The married-filing-separately control already shows
its own ceilings in that same counter on both cores, because taxable income
is blocked on the spouse questions; line 3 still publishes 15,750. That is
unchanged.

## 2. How refusal is read today

`readers.py` scans published 2025 content (`readers.json`).

The only computation rule that reads `tax.us.2025.hoh.tests-met` or
`tax.us.2025.qss.tests-met` is `tax.us.2025.rule.dependents-worksheet-line-3`
v2. It references each fact inside the matching filing-status branch. It
does not put either fact in `requires`, so a single, joint, or separate
return does not need the answer. The facts are also named by their input
questions and by `filing-status-tests.bundle.json`.

`tax.us.2025.parameter.preferential-zero-ceiling` is read by
`rule.qdcg-worksheet.line-6` v1. `tax.us.2025.parameter.preferential-fifteen-ceiling`
is read by `rule.qdcg-worksheet.line-13` v1. Both rules require only
`filing_status` and look the ceiling up from it.

The only direct reader of line 6 is `rule.qdcg-worksheet.line-7` (requires
and ref). The only direct reader of line 13 is `rule.qdcg-worksheet.line-14`.
Published symbols downstream of line 6 are worksheet lines 7, 8, 9, 11, 12,
15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 23, and 25. Downstream of line 13 are lines 14,
16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 23, and 25. Line 10 reads line 4. Lines 22 and 24
require `filing_status` and figure the tax on line 5 and on line 1. They do
not read the ceilings.

Lines 6 and 13 can read the same answers line 3 reads. The scratch
successors do, and `generate_to_directory` accepted them on schemas v102
already admits.

## 3. What the 2025 instructions say

Source: 2025 Instructions for Form 1040,
`https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-prior/i1040gi--2025.pdf`, sha256
`482e9c487c608f1bbeaceef35bc3c0933e8b35443cfff447e4279d590468364a`.
Line numbers are a `pdftotext` extraction without layout (`law.json`). The
same PDF hash is recorded in `hoh-eic-evidence/p0.md`.

Filing Status (967-982): "Check only the filing status that applies to you."
"You can choose the one for which you qualify that will give you the lowest
tax."

Head of household (1126-1146): the box is for a filer who is unmarried and
provides a home for certain other persons. "Check the “Head of household”
box only if you are unmarried (or considered unmarried) and either Test 1
or Test 2 applies."

Qualifying surviving spouse (1270-1291): the box applies, and joint-return
tax rates apply, if all of the listed conditions apply. "If your spouse died
in 2025, you can't file as qualifying surviving spouse."

Qualified Dividends and Capital Gain Tax Worksheet—Line 16 (4002). Before
you begin: complete Form 1040 or 1040-SR through line 15 (4037-4038). Line 1
is that taxable income.

Line 6 (4057-4061): "Enter: $48,350 if single or married filing separately,
$96,700 if married filing jointly or qualifying surviving spouse, $64,750 if
head of household." Line 7 is the smaller of line 1 or line 6.

Line 13 (4068-4073): "Enter: $533,400 if single, $300,000 if married filing
separately, $600,050 if married filing jointly or qualifying surviving
spouse, $566,700 if head of household." Line 14 is the smaller of line 1 or
line 13.

The worksheet has no row for a checked status the filer does not qualify
for, and it does not say to enter another status's amount. The instructions'
answer is to check a status that applies. Lines 6 and 13 should block and
name the existing question. They should keep that status's ceiling when the
answer is yes.

## 4. Content route

`design.py` writes `rule.qdcg-worksheet.line-6.v2.json` and
`rule.qdcg-worksheet.line-13.v2.json`. Each keeps its id and scope, raises
the version to v2, and uses `rule-artifact.v17` so an expression block can
name the missing answer. v14, which the published rules use, cannot. v102
already admits v17.

The value is the published parameter lookup, wrapped the way line 3 v2 is
wrapped. When the filing status is head of household, yes keeps the lookup
and no is `DEPENDENCY_INVALID` naming `hoh.tests-met`. When it is qualifying
surviving spouse, the same shape names `qss.tests-met`. Every other status
keeps the v1 lookup and references neither fact. An unanswered answer fails
the yes comparison and blocks by naming the fact (`DEPENDENCY_ABSENT`),
which is what the live trace recorded.

`build.py` copies published content outside the repository, writes the two
files, and validates with `tools.package_successor.generate_to_directory`.
`build.json`: status valid. No other package member pins line 6 v1 or line
13 v1 (`replaced_rules_pinned_by` is empty). Rule sha256
`84e0093914d162830cb391d8fd808dee1200803c8c8ba457bde62baa87c665b9` (line 6,
4,224 bytes) and
`1c01c3eeccd59d4ab61dee9cc6f646ffabca62455e0c6b534946e8fee25e630e` (line 13,
4,251 bytes). Registry sha256
`5dbea8c39741b75b549eabc67f8a902218c33ba34465537dbee8b6ec3caa0b81`.

An unrelated path does not need a new answer. The final branch is the v1

No engine, schema, or projection change. Nothing under `packages/` or

## 7. Decisions for the foreman

1. Publish the content route. Block lines 6 and 13 the way line 3 blocks.
   Do not publish another status's ceiling. The worksheet assigns a ceiling
   to each filing status, and the filing-status instructions say to check
   only a status that applies.
2. Version. Scratch v103 / registry v101 / release v101 is the successor of
   published v102 used here. eic-separated-married is using v103. Track 1
   publishes the next free core after it.
3. The fast size guard's worksheet filename set does not include the v2
   files. Both are under the 6,144-byte cap. Track 1 admits the two
   filenames. That test edit is outside P0.
4. Lines 22 and 24 read filing status for the tax on line 5 and line 1.
   They do not read the ceilings. On a refused status they stay blocked
   because taxable income is absent. Leave them as they are.
5. Single, joint, and separate returns keep the v1 lookup and do not read
   the test answers. The live controls and the 775-list replay show no new
   question and no number change.
6. No engine, schema, or projection change. `rule-artifact.v17` is already
   admitted on v102, and the successors use it so the block can name the
   answer.
7. No page change. The existing status question stays on the blocked
   deduction and tax lines. After the successor the refused ceiling is no
   longer in the explanation graph.

```
python3 build.py <directory-outside-the-repository>
python3 trace.py --core-json <scratch-core.json> --out trace-v103.json
python3 sizes.py <scratch-content> --out sizes.json
python3 replay.py --census --out census.json
python3 replay.py <scratch-core.json> --shard 0/4 --part <part-file>
python3 replay.py <scratch-core.json> --summarize <part-files> --out replay.json
```

inside the repository. The sibling head-of-household probe and the audit
harness are loaded by path under unique module names.
