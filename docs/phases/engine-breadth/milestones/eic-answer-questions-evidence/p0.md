## Result in one paragraph

Core v99 has ten credit answer rules, not eleven: `rule.eic-answer.no-qualifying-child`
is in the content directory but is not a v99 member. Six have an input
question. Four do not: the age answer (`eic.age-25-under-65`), the main-home
answer (`eic.main-home-in-us`), the not-claimable-as-dependent answer
(`eic.not-claimable-as-dependent`) and the spouse SSN answer
(`eic.spouse-valid-ssn`). A filer who must give any of them and has not is
blocked on lines 27a, 32, 33, 34, 35a and 37, and the saved page shows no
sentence on any of those lines. This was reproduced for single, qualifying
surviving spouse, unmarried head of household and joint filers. Nothing on
the record proves any of the four answers soundly. The smallest honest
change is content only: four `input-question.v1` members naming the
existing fact types. No rule reads anything new and no path needs a
different answer. On the scratch v100 every one of those blocked lines shows
the missing question(s) as its only sentence. Answered returns keep every
value. All 775 recorded lists and all 32 probe lists pass the strict
full-surface replay with only declared changes. One related gap is outside
this design. A counted child's roster answer (age test, residency) that is
missing also blocks the credit with no sentence, and a content question
cannot reach that block (Decision 3). **Recommendation: publish the four
questions as core v100, registry v98, release v98.**

A missing unasked answer blocks 11 rules: the answer rule, the gate result,
eligibility, line 27a, the 27-31 total and lines 32-37. Each of the six
page lines 27a, 32, 33, 34, 35a and 37 shows no sentence on v99.
`missing_answers_named` in each run lists the fact the blocked rows name.

**Why the page is silent.** The page attaches a question to a blocked
line only when every root the line waits on is a family with a closure
question or a fact with a declared `input-question`
(`presentation_projection._closure_question_reasons`). These four facts
have none, so nothing is attached. When several answers are missing, each
must have a question, or no sentence shows at all. That is why all four are
needed together.

**Why a child roster answer cannot be fixed the same way.** With a child's
age answer missing, `rule.eic-child.age-met` publishes nothing for the
child, `rule.eic-child.counts` blocks naming `eic.child.age-met`, and
`rule.eic-qualifying-child-count` blocks `DEPENDENCY_INVALID` naming the
member key (`eic.child.relationship|child=...`). The page's walk stops at a
root that is neither a family nor a fact type with a question. An
`input-question` for `eic.child.age` would not be reached. Fixing it needs
a projection change, or a roster-member question mechanism (Decision 3).

## 3. What the record proves

| Answer | Candidate on the record | Sound? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| age 25 to 64 | `taxpayer-over-65` (born before January 2, 1961), `spouse-over-65` | **No.** A filer born January 1, 1961 is 65 for the standard deduction and still meets the credit's test ("born after December 31, 1960", i1040gi 4533-4534). On a joint return either spouse can meet it (p596 1697-1705). No fact proves the lower bound (age 25). No date of birth is recorded. |
| main home in the United States | none (`f1098.shared-main-home` is about a mortgage) | No fact. |
| not claimable as a dependent | `standard-deduction.can-be-claimed-as-dependent` (the line 12a "Someone can claim you" box) | **Sound in meaning** for a non-joint return: Pub. 596 Rule 12 says "If you aren't filing a joint return, you meet this rule if you did not check the box on line 12a that says 'Someone can claim you as a dependent'" (p596 1753-1755). **Not simple**: the rule language has no op that uses a recorded explicit answer when present and falls back otherwise (`conditional_dependency_set` blocks when absent). A rule that reads the line 12a answer instead would change which answer the path needs. Where the two answers disagree, it would also change a number for a filer who has already answered. Rejected here (Decision 2). |
| spouse SSN | `senior-deduction.spouse-valid-ssn` (same test), `ctc.spouse-ssn-or-itin` and `odc.spouse-tin` (an ITIN also counts) | The senior-deduction answer is asked only for that deduction, so it is rarely present. The other two are not the credit's test. No sound, simple derivation. |

So all four answers are asked. No derivation is proposed.

## 4. Design

Content only (`design.py`, `build.py`): four `input-question.v1` members,
each naming one fact type of the published `tax.us.2025.eic-questions.vocabulary`
v1 by exact pin, each also an entrypoint, as v99's six are. No rule, bundle,
schema, projection, page or kernel change. No answer rule changes, so the
same answers are required on the same paths and every number is unchanged.

Sources: local copies (ignored `temp/`) of the 2025 Instructions for Form
1040 (i1040gi, sha256 `482e9c487c608f1bbeaceef35bc3c0933e8b35443cfff447e4279d590468364a`)
and the 2025 Publication 596 (p596, sha256
`29e8c6695912318253743210fcd68aaa10ad874c76edcab4aa7dc4b23ff51dbf`),
the same files as the hoh-eic P0. Line numbers are of the same `pdftotext`
extractions.

**`input-question.eic-age-25-under-65`** (polarity pos: yes records yes)

> Were you, or your spouse if you file a joint return, at least age 25 but
> under age 65 at the end of 2025? Answer yes if you, or your spouse if you
> file a joint return, were born after December 31, 1960, and before January
> 2, 2001. If you or your spouse died in 2025, answer yes if the person who
> died was at least age 25 but under age 65 at the time of death (a person
> reaches age 25 on the day before their 25th birthday, and age 65 on their
> 65th birthday).

Source, line 27a Step 4 question 2 (i1040gi 4531-4543): "Were you, or your
spouse if filing a joint return, at least age 25 but under age 65 at the end
of 2025? (Check "Yes" if you, or your spouse if filing a joint return, were
born after December 31, 1960, and before January 2, 2001.) If your spouse
died in 2025 or if you are preparing a return for someone who died in 2025,
see Pub. 596 before you answer." Pub. 596 Rule 11 (p596 1719-1749): "you
meet the age test if you are at least age 25 but under age 65 at the end of
2025, or your spouse was at least age 25 but under age 65 at the time of
death"; "Your spouse is considered to reach age 25 on the day before their
25th birthday. However, the rule for reaching age 65 is different; your
spouse reaches age 65 on their 65th birthday"; "A taxpayer who died in 2025
meets the age test if the taxpayer was at least age 25 but under age 65 at
the time of death."

**`input-question.eic-main-home-in-us`** (pos)

> Was your main home, and your spouse's if you file a joint return, in the
> United States for more than half of 2025? Your main home can be any place
> where you regularly live, such as a house, apartment, shelter or temporary
> lodging, and it need not be the same place all year. The United States
> means the 50 states and the District of Columbia, not Puerto Rico or a U.S.
> territory. If you were on extended active military duty outside the United
> States, count that time as time your main home was in the United States.

Source, Step 4 question 3 (i1040gi 4548-4556): "Was your main home, and your
spouse's if filing a joint return, in the United States for more than half
of 2025? Your main home can be any location where you regularly live."
Definitions: "Main home. Your main home may be your house, apartment, mobile
home, shelter, temporary lodging, or other location and doesn't need to be
the same physical location throughout 2025" (4795-4797); "If you were on
extended active duty outside the United States, your main home is considered
to be in the United States during that duty period" (4802-4804); "United
States. The United States means the 50 states and the District of Columbia.
It doesn't include Puerto Rico or U.S. ter[ritories]" (4911-4912; p596
1918-1919).

**`input-question.eic-not-claimable-as-dependent`** (polarity abs: yes
records no, as the published EIC3 question does)

> Can you be claimed as a dependent on someone else's 2025 tax return (do not
> count the case where the person who could claim you is not required to file
> a 2025 return and is not filing one, or is filing only to claim a refund of
> withheld income tax or estimated tax paid)?

Source, Step 4 question 6 (i1040gi 4583-4589): "Can you be claimed as a
dependent on someone else's 2025 tax return? (If the person who could claim
you on their 2025 tax return is not required to file, and isn't filing a 2025
tax return or is filing a 2025 return only to claim a refund of withheld
income tax or estimated tax paid, check "No.")" The rule asks it only when
the return is not joint, as Step 4 question 4 skips questions 5 and 6 for a
joint return (4565-4569).

**`input-question.eic-spouse-valid-ssn`** (pos; worded as the published
EIC1 taxpayer question)

> Does your spouse's Social Security number allow your spouse to work in the
> United States? Answer yes if it was issued by the Social Security
> Administration on or before the due date of your 2025 return (including
> extensions) and is not marked "Not Valid for Employment", or if it is
> marked "Valid for Work Only With DHS Authorization" and that authorization
> is still valid.

Source, Step 1 question 2 (i1040gi 4368-4370, continued at 4259-4262 in the
extraction's column order): "Do you, and your spouse if filing a joint
return, have a social security number issued on or before the due date of
your 2025 return (including extensions) that allows you to work and is valid
for EIC purposes (explained later under Definitions and Special Rules)?"
Definitions, "Social security number (SSN)" (4876-4889): a valid SSN is one
issued by the SSA "unless 'Not Valid for Employment' is printed on the social
security card and the number was issued solely to allow the recipient ... to
apply for or receive a federally funded benefit"; "if 'Valid for Work Only
With DHS Authorization' is printed ... your SSN is valid for EIC purposes
only as long as the DHS authorization is still valid".

**Package.** Core v99 to v100: four members and four entrypoints added,
nothing replaced (`build.json`). `generate_to_directory` validated the
package, registry v98 and release v98. Every question names a fact type
that the v99 bundle declares and that no v99 question already names
(`build.py` asserts both).

**Rejected shapes.**

- Derivations (section 3): none is both sound and simple.
- Asking the not-claimable question positively ("Is it true that no one can
  claim you ..."): it departs from the instructions' wording and the
  published EIC3 sibling. See Decision 1.
- Pointing the age question at Pub. 596 ("see Pub. 596 before you answer"):
  a filer can act on the death rule only if it is stated, so it is stated.

## 6. Recommendation

Publish the four input questions as core v100, registry v98, release v98,
content only (plus the usual v100 loader entries carried from v99 in
`packages/tax/loader.py`, as every core successor has). Track 1: pin the
probe cases live on v100, the recorded-list replay as the neighbour check,
and a test that each question's polarity matches its fact (as
`tests/test_scope_answer_questions.py` does for EIC1-EIC4). No engine,
schema, projection, page or kernel change, and no new ADR.

## 7. Decisions for the foreman

1. **Polarity of the not-claimable question.** It follows Step 4 question 6
   and the published EIC3: yes means "can be claimed" and records the fact's
   no. An entry surface must invert it, as for EIC2-EIC4. Recommended: keep
   the instructions' wording (abs). The alternative is a positive rewording.
2. **Deriving the not-claimable answer from the line 12a answer** (Pub. 596
   Rule 12). The meaning matches for a non-joint return, and every recorded
   list agrees. But content cannot prefer the filer's explicit answer, so a
   derivation would change which answer the path needs. Recommended: not in
   this milestone; park "ask the line 12a box once" as a separate dedupe.
3. **Per-child roster answers.** A counted child's missing age or residency
   answer (and, by the same path, presumably SSN and joint-return) blocks
   the credit and the refund lines with no sentence, on v99 and v100 alike.
   A content question cannot reach it: the count rule blocks
   `DEPENDENCY_INVALID` on a member key. Fixing it needs a projection change
   or a roster-member question. That is a stop condition for this content
   design, so it is reported, not built. Recommended: park it as its own
   milestone (it likely affects the child tax credit roster the same way),
   or widen this milestone by your decision.
4. **The age question states the death rules** of Pub. 596 Rule 11 instead
   of "see Pub. 596". Check against the source before Track 1 (plan Decision 1).
5. **Plan text.** The plan says eleven credit answer rules, five possibly
   unasked, including no-qualifying-child. v99 has ten, four unasked.
   `rule.eic-answer.no-qualifying-child` is not a v99 member, and its fact
   is not read. Correct the plan's opening paragraph.
