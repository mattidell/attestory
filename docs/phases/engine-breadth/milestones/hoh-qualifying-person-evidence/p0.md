## Result in one paragraph

On core v97 a filer who answers head of household or qualifying surviving
spouse gets that status's standard deduction, Tax Table column and
preferential-rate ceilings with nothing else asked: no qualifying person,
home cost, marital status or year of the spouse's death is recorded or read,
and a return with no child or dependent at all computes a larger refund than
the same return filed single (by 1,050 on a 50,000 wage return as head of
household, 2,022 as qualifying surviving spouse). No accepted ADR or rule note
bounds this. The record never proves either status (home cost, marital status
and the spouse's year of death are not recorded anywhere) and never proves a
contradiction either (a qualifying person need not be a claimed dependent),
so the smallest honest change is a question, not an inference: one
return-level yes/no per status, read only on that status by the one rule that
looks up the standard deduction base for the filing status. Yes keeps every
number; no refuses line 12e and everything that reads it and shows the
question beside the refusal; unanswered asks it. Content only: one new
vocabulary bundle, two input questions and one rule successor (on the already
published `rule-artifact.v17`, ADR-0086), no engine, schema, projection, page
or kernel change. All 775 recorded lists are single filers, and the full
replay changes nothing on them but the package and rule versions.
**Recommendation: publish variant q as core v98, registry v96, release v96.**

## 1. Trace: what each status changes on core v97

The status is one return-level answer, `tax.us.2025.filing-status`, with
five values (`packages/content/tax/2025/core_calculations.bundle.v4.json:4`,
`:20-27`), bound to the symbol `filing_status` as a required package input
(`package.core-calculations.v97.json`, `input_bindings`). Its only question
is "What is your filing status for 2025: single, married filing jointly,
married filing separately, head of household, or qualifying surviving
spouse?" (`input-question.filing-status.v1.json:7`). Nothing else is asked
about either status.

Twenty-seven v97 members read it (rules by `filing_status` or the fact id).

complete and silently too favourable**: the same 50,000 return is refunded
1,675 as head of household and 2,647 as qualifying surviving spouse against

The qss/none return is identical, line for line, to the joint return.

A second observation, against the filer and outside this milestone's
direction: `rule.eic-status-class` puts every head of household in class 0, so
a head of household with no qualifying child gets no earned income credit
(hoh/relative/low: 0, where single/none/low gets 542). The 2025 instructions
stop only a *married* head of household (line 27a Step 4 question 1, "Are you
a married taxpayer whose filing status is married filing separately or head
of household?", `i1040gi-2025` raw text lines 4517-4527). An unmarried head of
household whose qualifying person is a dependent parent or relative is
entitled to the credit with no qualifying child. See Decision 5.

## 2. Sources

Local copies (ignored `temp/`): the 2025 Instructions for Form 1040 PDF was
already local (sha256 below, the same file the bartering P0 cited); the 2025
Publication 501 was not, and was fetched from the IRS prior-year directory.

| Document | sha256 (PDF) |
| --- | --- |

**Head of household** (i1040gi lines 1126-1214, 1229-1250; Pub. 501 lines
1084-1110, 1111-1170, 1171-1253, 1298-1330, Table 4 at 1358):

1. *Unmarried or considered unmarried on December 31, 2025.* Considered
   unmarried: legally separated under a decree of divorce or separate
   maintenance; married but lived apart from the spouse for the last 6 months
   of 2025, filing separately, paying over half the cost of the home, which was
   the main home of the filer's child, stepchild or foster child for more than
   half the year, whom the filer can claim (or could but for the noncustodial
   parent rule); or a nonresident alien spouse with no election.
2. *Paid over half the cost of keeping up a home for 2025* (Pub. 501
   Worksheet 1: rent, mortgage interest, taxes, insurance, repairs, utilities,
   food eaten in the home).
3. *A qualifying person.* Test 1: a parent the filer can claim as a dependent
   (not under a multiple support agreement) whose main home for all of 2025
   the filer paid over half of; the parent need not live with the filer.
   Test 2: in the filer's home for more than half of 2025, any person the
   filer can claim as a dependent (except a child claimed only under the
   divorced-parents rule, a person who is a dependent only because they lived
   with the filer all year, and a multiple-support dependent), or the filer's
   unmarried qualifying child who is not the filer's dependent, or certain
   married or custodial-parent qualifying children who are not dependents.
   Table 4 adds that a qualifying relative other than a parent counts only if
   related in one of the listed ways and living with the filer more than half
   the year, and that one person cannot qualify two filers.

**Qualifying surviving spouse** (i1040gi lines 1270-1300 and the continuation
"3. This child lived in your home ... 5. You could have filed a joint return"
at 1255-1265; Pub. 501 lines 1478-1560):

1. The spouse died in 2023 or 2024, and the filer did not remarry before the
   end of 2025 (a 2025 death files jointly instead; i1040gi line 1289).
2. The filer could have filed a joint return for the year of death.
3. A child or stepchild (not a foster child) the filer can claim as a
   dependent, or could except that the child had gross income of $5,200 or
   more, filed a joint return, or the filer could be claimed as someone
   else's dependent.
4. That child lived in the filer's home all of 2025 (temporary absences,
   birth, death and adoption exceptions).
5. The filer paid over half the cost of keeping up that home.

**What the engine records or can ask:**

| Test | Recorded on v97? |
| --- | --- |
| Marital status at year end; lived apart last 6 months | No. The EIC's separated-spouse answer (`eic.separated-spouse`) is a different test (EIC Step 3: not married, or no shared principal residence for the last 6 months, or legally separated under a written agreement), asked only with an EIC qualifying child |
| Paid over half the cost of the home | No |
| Qualifying person lived with the filer more than half the year | Partly: a CTC child's "lived with you for more than half of 2025" and "claimed as dependent" answers, the EIC child's residency answer; nothing for a relative's residence (the relative roster asks relationship, support, gross income, TIN, citizenship, joint return); nothing for a parent's main home |
| Can claim the person as a dependent | Partly: CTC `claimed-as-dependent`, ODC relative box, `ctc.no-other-dependent` |
| Year of the spouse's death; no remarriage; could have filed jointly | No |
| QSS child lived in the home all year | No (the CTC answer is "more than half") |

The record therefore never proves either status: home cost and marital
status (and for QSS the spouse's year of death) are recorded nowhere. Nor
does it prove a contradiction: a HoH qualifying person may be a qualifying
child who is not claimed (custodial parent who released the claim on Form
8332, or a filer who simply may claim but has not listed the person), or a
dependent parent the filer has not entered on the relative roster; a QSS
child may be one the filer cannot claim because of the $5,200, joint-return
or filer-is-a-dependent exceptions. A refusal inferred from "no child or
dependent recorded" would refuse filers who qualify.

**Bounds.** No accepted ADR names head of household or qualifying surviving
spouse qualification (ADR index digests; the only ADR text naming the
statuses is ADR-0058 listing the capital-loss limit by status). The filing
status fact title is only "Filing status for tax year 2025". No rule note
claims the statuses were tested. ADR-0086 (`rule-artifact.v17`, an expression
`block` may name the answer it refuses on, explanation routing only) is the
contract the design uses to show the question beside a "no".

## 3. Design

**Recommended (variant q).** Content only (`design.py`):

- A new vocabulary bundle `tax.us.2025.filing-status-tests.vocabulary` v1
  (`filing-status-tests.bundle.json`) with two return-level yes/no facts,
  `tax.us.2025.hoh.tests-met` and `tax.us.2025.qss.tests-met`, whose titles
  state every test above with citations and say what yes and no mean.
- Two input questions, `input-question.hoh-tests-met` v1 and
  `input-question.qss-tests-met` v1:

  > You chose head of household. Answer yes only if all three of these
  > apply. (1) You were unmarried on December 31, 2025, or you were
  > married but your spouse did not live in your home at any time during
  > the last 6 months of 2025 and your child, stepchild or foster child
  > whom you can claim as a dependent (or could claim except that the
  > child's other parent claims them) lived in your home for more than
  > half of 2025. If your spouse died in 2025, you count as married for
  > all of 2025 and cannot answer yes. (2) You paid more than half the
  > cost of keeping up your home for 2025, or, if your qualifying person
  > is your dependent parent, more than half the cost of your parent's
  > main home for all of 2025. (3) A qualifying person lived with you for
  > more than half of 2025 (a dependent parent need not live with you):
  > your qualifying child, or a relative you can claim as a dependent who
  > is your parent, brother, sister, half brother, half sister,
  > stepbrother, stepsister, grandparent, aunt, uncle, niece, nephew or
  > in-law. Someone who is your dependent only because they lived with you
  > all year, or whom you claim only under a multiple support agreement,
  > is not a qualifying person. If any of these does not apply, you cannot
  > file as head of household: change your filing status (if you were
  > married, that is usually married filing jointly or married filing
  > separately).

  > You chose qualifying surviving spouse. Answer yes only if all of these
  > apply: your spouse died in 2023 or 2024 and you did not remarry before
  > the end of 2025; you could have filed a joint return with your spouse
  > for the year your spouse died; you have a child or stepchild (not a
  > foster child) whom you can claim as a dependent, or could claim except
  > that the child had gross income of $5,200 or more, filed a joint
  > return, or you can be claimed as someone else's dependent; that child
  > lived in your home all of 2025, apart from temporary absences such as
  > school (a child born or who died in 2025 counts if the child lived
  > there for the part of the year the child was alive); and you paid more
  > than half the cost of keeping up that home for 2025. If any of these
  > does not apply, you cannot file as a qualifying surviving spouse:
  > change your filing status (if your spouse died in 2025, you can file a
  > joint return for 2025).

- `rule.dependents-worksheet-line-3` v1 to v2 (schema `rule-artifact.v14` to
  `rule-artifact.v17`): on head of household it reads the head of household
  answer, on qualifying surviving spouse the qualifying surviving spouse
  answer. Yes publishes exactly v1's lookup; no is
  `{"op": "block", "code": "DEPENDENCY_INVALID", "missing": [<the answer>]}`;
  unanswered blocks naming the answer. Single, married filing jointly and
  married filing separately take v1's expression unchanged and read nothing
  new. Nothing pins the v1 rule, so the replacement cascades to nothing.

Package changes: replace one member, add three members and three entrypoints
(`build-q.json`). `generate_to_directory` validated the package, registry and
release.

**What a filer sees** (`probe-v98-*.json`):

- Unanswered: line 12e, 14, 15, 16, 19, 24, 28, 33-37 block, and each shows
  the status question (one sentence, the only question on the page). Line 11
  (AGI) and line 27a still compute; neither differs between these statuses
  and single.
- Yes: every watched line, disposition and value is identical to v97, on all
  six head of household and qualifying surviving spouse cases.
- No: the same lines block and show the same question, whose last sentence
  says the status does not apply and to change it. Line 12e's own field
  sentence still reads "Line 12e has no value yet. It is waiting for your
  standard deduction and your itemized deductions." (field v2, unchanged), so
  the page does not distinguish "no" from "unanswered" except by the recorded
  answer (Decision 2).
- Single, married filing jointly and the controls: identical to v97.

**Rejected shapes.**

- Refusing from the record (no CTC child, no other dependent): refuses
  qualifying filers whose qualifying person is not a claimed dependent
  (section 2), and still does not ask home cost or marital status.
- Skipping the question when a CTC child is counted: the child proves at most
  the qualifying-person test; home cost and marital status still need the
  filer, so the question is still needed and a shorter one would add a second
  question citizen for little gain.
- Gating line 12e, line 15 or the tax rules instead: more rule successors for
  the same reach; line 3 is the single point every status-dependent amount
  passes through.
- Treating a "no" as single: the engine would be choosing a filing status for
  the filer (a married filer who fails head of household files married
  filing separately, not single); refusing and naming the question leaves
  that choice with the filer.
- One question per test (three for HoH, five for QSS): more answers on every
  qualifying return with no number depending on which test failed; possible
  later if the owner wants the refusal to name the failing test.

## 5. Recommendation

Publish variant q as core v98, registry v96, release v96: the bundle, two
questions and the line 3 rule successor, content only. It changes no number on
any recorded return (all single), keeps every number for a filer who answers
yes, refuses the larger amounts for a filer who answers no, and asks the
filer who chose either status and has not answered. Track 1: pin the probe
cases live on v98 (each status unanswered, yes and no; the single and joint
controls unchanged; one itemizing head of household return to show line 12e
blocks on the itemized route too), plus the recorded-list replay as the
neighbour check. No engine, schema, projection, page or kernel change is
needed.

## 6. Decisions for the foreman

1. **One combined question per status, or one per test.** Recommended:
   one per status. Per-test answers would let the refusal name the failing
   test at the cost of three (HoH) or five (QSS) answers on every qualifying
   return.
2. **Line 12e's blocked sentence after a "no".** The page shows the question
   (with its consequence sentence) but the field still says the line is
   "waiting". A line 12e form-field successor whose blocked sentence also
   covers a refused status would change line 12e's field version and sentence
   on every saved page (all 775 recorded lists). Recommended: leave it; the
   question carries the reason.
3. **Head of household with a counted CTC child still answers the question.**
   The record proves at most the qualifying-person test; home cost and
   marital status are not recorded. Accept, or narrow the question's wording
   when a child is counted (a second question citizen and a branch).
4. **Qualifying surviving spouse "lived in your home all of 2025"** is
   stricter than any recorded child answer ("more than half"), so no record
   shortcut exists for it.
5. **Earned income credit for an unmarried head of household with no
   qualifying child** is $0 on v97 (`rule.eic-status-class`), where the 2025
   instructions stop only a married head of household. This errs against the
   filer and is outside this milestone (its scope is a status that is too
   favourable). Park it, or charter it separately: the fix needs a marital
   answer the engine does not record (the HoH question's "unmarried or
   considered unmarried" does not split the two).
6. **Placement of the refusal.** Line 11 (AGI) and line 27a (EIC) still
   compute on a refused or unanswered status. Both are the same for these
   statuses as for single (section 1), so they are not too favourable;
   accept.
