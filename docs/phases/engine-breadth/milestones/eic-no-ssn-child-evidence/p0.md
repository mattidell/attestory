## Result

A child who meets the relationship, age, residency, and joint-return tests is
a qualifying child for the earned income credit. A valid SSN is not one of
those tests. It decides whether that child raises the amount. The filer who
has such a child is a filer with a qualifying child, completes Schedule EIC,
and does not take the credit for a filer without a qualifying child.

A married filer who files as head of household or married filing separately
must meet the separated-spouse rule. The 2025 instructions put that test in
Step 3, questions 3 through 5, and then skip Step 4. Step 4 is only for a
filer without a qualifying child, and its first question stops a married
head of household or a married filing separately return. Publication 596
states the same special rule, and says a qualifying child without a valid
SSN can still support a self-only credit.

On published v101 the amount column is the count of qualifying children with
a valid SSN. A child without one contributes 0, so a married filer whose only
qualifying child lacks a valid SSN is treated as having no qualifying child.
Head of household falls into class 1 and is asked whether they were married.
Married filing separately hits the early stop. Line 27a is $0. Worksheet A
line 6 can still be the no-child table amount.

The scratch successor keeps the SSN test on `rule.eic-child.counts` and adds
`rule.eic-child.qualifies`, the same tests without the SSN. A return-level
count of those children feeds status class, the early stop, whether questions
are required, and the separated-spouse and Step 4 skips. At wages 7,000 the
separated filer whose qualifying children have no valid SSN gets the
`other-0` table amount, 537. A qualifying child with a valid SSN keeps
`other-1`, 2,389, and the child without a valid SSN does not raise that
column. All 50 synthetic cases match that hand credit. All 775 recorded
lists pass the strict replay with the declared structural changes and no
change to a recorded number. No engine change and no new ADR.

**Recommendation: publish this content route as core v102, registry v100,
release v100.**

**Who is a qualifying child.** Instructions, printed page 41, lines
4322-4350: a qualifying child for the EIC is a son, daughter, stepchild,
foster child, brother, sister, stepbrother, stepsister, half brother, half
sister, or a descendant of any of them; under age 19 and younger than the
filer (or the spouse if filing jointly), or under age 24, a full-time
student, and younger, or any age and permanently and totally disabled; who
is not filing a joint return for 2025, or is filing one only to claim a
refund of withheld income tax or estimated tax paid; and who lived with the
filer in the United States for more than half of 2025. The definition has
no SSN test. Lines 4353-4355: the credit cannot be taken for a child who
did not live with the filer for more than half the year.

Publication 596, chapter 2, printed page 9, lines 796-813: follow Rules 8
through 10 if a child meets the conditions to be a qualifying child for the
EIC, even if that child does not have a valid SSN issued on or before the
due date of the 2025 return (including extensions). Attach Schedule EIC
even if that child does not have a valid SSN. "If you don't meet Rule 8,
you don't have a qualifying child. Read chapter 3." Rule 8, lines 823-832,
is the relationship, age, residency, and joint-return tests. The residency
test, printed page 11, lines 994-996, requires the child to have lived with
the filer in the United States for more than half of 2025. The instructions
repeat the Schedule EIC direction on printed page 40, lines 4221-4226.

**What the SSN changes.** Instructions, printed page 45, lines 4772-4778:
if a child did not have an SSN issued on or before the due date of the
return (including extensions), "you can't count that child as a qualifying
child in figuring the amount of the EIC" on the original or an amended
return. Step 1 question 1, printed page 40, lines 4242-4254, and Step 5
question 3, printed page 42, lines 4529-4541, set the income limits by how
many children who have valid SSNs lived with the filer, and by "No children
who have valid SSNs" or "No qualifying children who have valid SSNs."
Worksheet A, printed page 46, lines 4814-4817: use the column for the
filing status and the number of qualifying children who have valid SSNs.

Publication 596, Rule 2, printed page 4, lines 416-423: the qualifying
child must have a valid SSN issued on or before the due date (including
extensions) "for you to claim a higher EIC amount based on that child." If
at least one child meets the conditions to be a qualifying child and does
not have that SSN, "you may be eligible to claim a self-only EIC if you are
otherwise eligible." The same sentence is repeated at Rule 3, printed page
5, lines 513-516, and after the four tests, printed page 12, lines
1106-1139, which also says that when more than one qualifying child has a
valid SSN the higher amount uses only the children who have one. The earned
income limits, printed page 3, lines 251-263, and Rule 1, printed page 3,
lines 381-390, use the same four bands, ending at "if you don't have a
qualifying child who has a valid SSN." The table columns, printed page 3,
lines 288-292, follow filing status and the number of qualifying children
with valid SSNs.

**Separated spouses.** The charter names this Step 4. In the 2025
instructions it is Step 3, questions 3 through 5, printed page 41, lines
4382-4421. Question 3 asks whether the filer is a married taxpayer whose
filing status is married filing separately or head of household. If not,
questions 4 and 5 are skipped. Question 4 asks whether the filer and spouse
had the same principal residence for the last 6 months of 2025. An answer
of no skips question 5. Question 5 asks whether the filer is legally
separated under state law under a written separation agreement or a decree
of separate maintenance and lived apart from the spouse at the end of 2025.
An answer of no stops the credit. Continuing past those questions, question
6 asks whether the filer could be a qualifying child of another person. An
answer of no checks the separated-spouse box on page 1 of Form 1040 or
1040-SR, skips Step 4, and goes to Step 5.

The prose special rule, printed page 44, lines 4686-4698, states the same
two prongs without the flowchart: a married filer who is not filing a joint
return, who had a qualifying child who lived with them in the United States
for more than half of 2025, can claim the EIC if they lived apart from
their spouse for the last 6 months of 2025, or they are legally separated
under a written separation agreement or a decree of separate maintenance
and did not live in the same household at the end of 2025. Either prong is
enough. Failing both stops the credit. Question 6 remains a separate stop
on that path.

Publication 596, Rule 3, printed page 5, lines 492-516, matches that prose.
The filer must have had a qualifying child who lived with them for more
than half of 2025, and either lived apart for the last 6 months of 2025 or
be legally separated under a written separation agreement or a decree of
separate maintenance and not have lived in the same household at the end of
2025. Schedule EIC is attached whether or not the qualifying child has a
valid SSN. A child without a valid SSN may still support a self-only EIC.

**The credit for a filer without a qualifying child.** Step 4, printed
pages 41-42, lines 4422-4481, is "Filers Without a Qualifying Child."
Question 1: a married taxpayer whose filing status is married filing
separately or head of household stops and cannot take the credit. The
later questions (age 25 but under 65, main home in the United States, not
a qualifying child of another person, not claimable as a dependent) are
reached only by a filer who is not that married taxpayer. Publication 596
sends a person who does not meet Rule 8 to chapter 3, and it sends a person
whose qualifying child is given to someone else by the tiebreaker rules to
the same chapter. A filer who has a qualifying child, including one without
a valid SSN, stays in chapter 2 and does not take that no-child credit.
Their amount, when no qualifying child has a valid SSN, is the self-only
column.

## Decisions for the foreman

1. Publish this route as core v102, registry v100, release v100. It is
   content on the accepted per-subject dependency contract. It needs no
   engine change and no new ADR.
2. Single, qualifying surviving spouse, and married filing jointly returns
