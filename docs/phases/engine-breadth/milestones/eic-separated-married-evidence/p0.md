# P0: The separated-spouse question is asked only of a married filer

P0 of eic-separated-married. Plan:
`docs/phases/engine-breadth/milestones/eic-separated-married.md`.
On milestone/eic-separated-married-2025, published core v102 (registry v100, release v100). Nothing in packages/ or
tests/ was edited.

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `hand.py` | The 2025 EIC table lookup and the credit the instructions give each synthetic case |
| `probe.py` | Twenty-one synthetic demo.* returns through `live_coordinate_run` |

## Result

Published core v102 cannot tell whether a head of household with a qualifying
child was married at the end of 2025. The separated-spouse question is asked
of every such filer. Answering it no makes line 27a $0. The 2025 instructions
ask Step 3 questions 4 and 5 only after question 3, and question 3 is no for
an unmarried head of household, so that filer keeps the table credit and is
not asked those questions.

The one recorded answer that does say a head of household was unmarried is
`tax.us.2025.eic.not-married`. On v102 it is asked only when no qualifying
child is present. Its published words are Step 4 question 1, and they do not
match Step 3 question 3 for a person who has a decree of separate maintenance
and still shared a household at the end of 2025. `hoh.tests-met` yes covers
an unmarried filer and a married filer who lived apart for the last six
months. Filing status `head_of_household` is both of those filers.

No scratch successor was built. No full-surface replay was run. No engine
change and no new ADR.

**Stop. The smallest new question is Step 3 question 3, asked of a head of
household who has a qualifying child.** Married filing separately is already
a married taxpayer by filing status, so that status does not need the new
question. A yes continues to questions 4 and 5. A no skips them and keeps
the credit the table gives.

> 3. Are you a married taxpayer whose filing status is married filing separately or head of household?

Question 6, lines 4496-4515, remains on the path that continues. An answer
of no checks the separated-spouse box and skips Step 4.

Step 4 question 1, printed page 41, lines 4522-4527, is the same sentence
as question 3, for a filer without a qualifying child. Yes stops the credit.

**Special rule for separated spouses.** Instructions, printed page 44,
lines 4818-4828:

> Special rule for separated spouses. If you are married, but not filing a joint return, had a qualifying child who lived with you in the United States for more than half of 2025, and either of the following apply, you can claim the EIC if:
> • You lived apart from your spouse for the last 6 months of 2025, or
> • You are legally separated according to your state law under a written separation agreement or a decree of separate maintenance and you didn't live in the same household as your spouse at the end of 2025.

Either prong is enough. The section is addressed to a person who is married.

Publication 596, printed page 4, lines 338-341, sends a married person who
does not file a joint return to Rule 3. Rule 3, printed page 5, lines
531-537: "If you are married, you must usually file a joint return to claim
the EIC. However, there is a special rule for separated spouses." The
special rule, printed page 6, lines 542-568, matches the instructions: married,
not filing a joint return, a qualifying child who lived with the filer for
more than half of 2025, and either the last-six-months prong or the
written-separation-or-decree prong with a different household at the end of
2025. Schedule EIC is attached whether or not that child has a valid SSN,
and a child without a valid SSN may still support a self-only credit.

The eligibility checklist, printed page 24, lines 2397-2398, asks question 3
as "Are you filing a joint return with your spouse or do you meet the special
rule for separated spouses?" and adds "Answer Yes if you weren't married at
the end of 2025."

**How "married" is judged for a head of household.** Instructions, Head of
Household, printed page 14, lines 1126-1143. A person may check head of
household if unmarried, and is considered unmarried for that purpose when
any of these applies: legally separated under a decree of divorce or
separate maintenance at the end of 2025 (an interlocutory decree still
counts as married); married but lived apart from the spouse for the last 6
months of 2025 and the other rules under Married persons who live apart are
met; or married to a nonresident alien for whom the resident election was
not made.

Married persons who live apart, printed page 15, lines 1229-1250, treats a
person who was not divorced or legally separated as unmarried for head of
household when all of these apply: lived apart for the last 6 months of
2025, file a separate return, paid over half the cost of keeping up the
home, the home was the main home of the child for more than half of 2025,
and the filer can claim that child (or could, except that the other parent
claims the child under the divorced-or-separated-parents rule).

Publication 596, printed page 5, lines 454-460, still calls that person
married for the credit: "If you are married, but qualify to file as head of
household or married filing separately under special rules for married
taxpayers living apart (see Rule 3)..." The six-month separation that makes
someone considered unmarried for head of household leaves them married for
Rule 3. They answer Step 3 question 3 yes, and question 4 no, because they
did not share a principal residence for the last 6 months.

A decree of separate maintenance is on both sides of the instructions. It
is one way to be considered unmarried for head of household (printed page
14, lines 1134-1138), and it is also a prong of Step 3 question 5, which is
reached only after question 3 is yes and which also requires that the filer
lived apart at the end of 2025. A written separation agreement, without a
decree, is a question 5 prong and is not one of the considered-unmarried
bullets.

## What the record knows

On published core v102 the separated-spouse answer is
`rule.eic-answer.separated-spouse` v2
(`packages/content/tax/2025/rule.eic-answer.separated-spouse.v2.json`).
It publishes 1 without reading an answer when questions are not required,
when the column is 0 and no qualifying child is present, or when the status
class is not 0 (lines 99-144). Otherwise it reads
`tax.us.2025.eic.separated-spouse` (lines 66-93). A no publishes 0.
`rule.eic-status-class` v3 puts a head of household in class 0 whenever a
qualifying child is present, including a child with no valid SSN
(`rule.eic-status-class.v3.json`, lines 74-100). Class 0 is also every
married filing separately return. The package pins those versions
(`package.core-calculations.v102.json`, the separated-spouse pin at line
4628 and the status-class pin at line 4742).

The input question for that fact,
`input-question.eic-separated-spouse.v1.json` line 7, tells the filer to
answer yes if they were not married at the end of 2025, or if either
statutory prong applies. A yes therefore does not say the filer was married.
The question is the one this milestone is trying to withhold from an
unmarried filer, so its own answer cannot decide whether to ask it.

The fact that does say whether a head of household was unmarried is
`tax.us.2025.eic.not-married`.

| Piece | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Fact type | `tax.us.2025.eic.not-married` v1, declared on `eic-marital-status.bundle.json` |
| Input question | `input-question.eic-not-married.v1.json` line 7 |
| Rule that reads it | `rule.eic-answer.not-married` v2, lines 111-146, and only in the branch that publishes 0 or 1 from the answer |

`rule.eic-answer.not-married` v2 publishes 1 without reading the fact when
questions are not required, the column count is greater than 0, the filing
status is not head of household, or a qualifying child is present (lines
60-108). The notes say the v2 change exists so Step 4 question 1 is not
asked of a head of household who has a qualifying child. No other rule file
names `tax.us.2025.eic.not-married`.

It is not always asked of a head of household. The live runs ask it on both
head-of-household returns with no qualifying child, and on none of the
returns that have a qualifying child, even though those returns carry the
answer. Married filing separately is not asked it. Filing status already
makes that return a married taxpayer for question 3.

Its meaning does not match Step 3 question 3. The instruction sentence
behind it is Step 4 question 1, which uses the same words as question 3, but
the published question is a different sentence. It is addressed to a head of
household for whom no child is counted, and it asks "Were you unmarried at
the end of 2025?" Yes includes a decree of divorce or separate maintenance,
with no requirement that the filer lived apart at the end of 2025. Question
5 uses that decree only together with living apart at the end of 2025, and
only after question 3 was yes. A filer who has the decree and still shared
a household at year end is unmarried on the published fact. On the Step 3
flowchart that filer can be a married taxpayer whom question 5 stops.

`tax.us.2025.hoh.tests-met` does not separate the two head-of-household
filers either. `input-question.hoh-tests-met.v1.json` line 7 accepts yes
when the filer was unmarried on December 31, 2025, or was married and the
spouse did not live in the home during the last 6 months of 2025 and the
child lived there for more than half the year. The only rule that reads it
is `rule.dependents-worksheet-line-3` v2, for the standard deduction, not
for the credit. Filing status `head_of_household` is the status both filers
choose. `mfs-lived-apart-all-year` is the standard-deduction question for
married filing separately. The probe answers it yes on every such return.
It is not the credit's separated-spouse test, and a head of household is
not asked it.

## Hand credit

`parameter.eic-table-2025` v2, wages 7,000, earned income equal to AGI, the
non-joint column. The lower bound is inclusive and the upper bound is
exclusive.

| Column | Who | Table value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `other-0` | no qualifying child with a valid SSN | 537 |
| `other-1` | one qualifying child with a valid SSN | 2,389 |

A qualifying child here lived with the filer for more than half the year and
meets the age test. A child without a valid SSN is still a qualifying child
and does not raise the column. `apart` and `writing` are the two prongs.
The published fact records either prong as yes.

An unmarried head of household skips questions 4 and 5. The credit is 2,389
with a valid-SSN qualifying child and 537 with a qualifying child who has
no valid SSN, or 537 with no qualifying child when the remaining Step 4
answers are yes. A married head of household or a married filing separately
return with a qualifying child takes that same table amount when one prong
is met, and $0 when neither is. With no qualifying child, Step 4 question 1
stops both of those married filers at $0.

## Route

No content route on the facts v102 already records asks questions 4 and 5
only of a married filer.

`eic.separated-spouse` cannot be the gate. `eic.not-married` is absent from
the required record whenever a qualifying child is present, which is when
questions 4 and 5 are asked of a head of household. Requiring it on that
path would be a new question, and the existing question's words say that no
child is counted and treat a decree of separate maintenance as unmarried
without the year-end household test in question 5. `hoh.tests-met` yes is
true for both filers. Filing status distinguishes married filing separately,
and does not distinguish the two head-of-household filers.

Nothing in this P0 changes which answer an unrelated path needs, because no
successor was loaded. Single, married filing jointly, and qualifying
surviving spouse returns are outside question 3 once question 2 is yes or
the status is not married filing separately or head of household. Married
filing separately with a qualifying child is already inside questions 4 and
5 by filing status. The trace leaves that asking in place.

The smallest new question, from Step 3 question 3, asked of a head of
household when a qualifying child is present:

> Are you a married taxpayer whose filing status is married filing separately or head of household?

Filing status is already known, so the part the record lacks is "Are you a
married taxpayer?" A person who files as head of household because they
lived apart for the last six months answers yes. They are still married for
the credit, and question 4 then records that they did not share a principal
residence. A person who was not married at the end of 2025 answers no, skips
questions 4 and 5, and keeps the table credit. The checklist's own gloss for
that no is "Answer Yes if you weren't married at the end of 2025" on the
joint-or-separated question (Publication 596, printed page 24, line 2398).

## Decisions for the foreman

1. Do not publish a successor that skips the separated-spouse question from
   facts already on a v102 return. The record cannot tell a married head of
   household with a qualifying child from an unmarried one.
2. The smallest new question is Step 3 question 3, asked of a head of
   household who has a qualifying child. Married filing separately does not
   need a new fact. A yes continues to questions 4 and 5. A no skips them.
3. Do not reuse `eic.not-married` for that question. It is withheld when a
   qualifying child is present, its words assume no counted child, and a
   decree of separate maintenance is a yes even when the filer still shared
   a household at the end of 2025.
4. Do not treat `hoh.tests-met` as the marital fact. Yes covers both filers.
5. No engine change, schema change, page change, or new ADR is indicated.
   Replay waits until a successor exists. At wages 7,000 the unmarried head
   of household the successor would stop asking keeps 2,389 with one
   valid-SSN qualifying child and 537 with a qualifying child who has no
   valid SSN.
