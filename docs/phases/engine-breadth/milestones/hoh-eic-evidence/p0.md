## Result in one paragraph

On core v98 `rule.eic-status-class` puts every head of household in class 0
with married filing separately, and the credit's early stop gives class 0
with no counted qualifying child $0 before any question is asked. A head of
household whose qualifying person is a dependent parent, or a child too old
for the credit, gets $0 where the same single return gets 537 (wages 7,000)
or 236 (wages 16,000). The 2025 instructions stop only a *married* taxpayer
filing as head of household (line 27a Step 4 question 1), and Publication 596
treats a married filer who is "considered unmarried" for head of household as
married for the credit. Nothing on the v98 record separates an unmarried head
of household from a married one: `hoh.tests-met` yes covers both, the
separated-spouse answer is asked only with a counted child and its yes also
covers both, and a counted child takes the filer off Step 4 altogether. So
the honest change is one new question, asked only of a head of household
with no counted qualifying child when the credit could otherwise be more than
$0. Content only: one bundle, one input question, a new
`rule.eic-answer.not-married`, `rule.eic-status-class` v2 (a head of
household with no counted child is class 1, as single) and
`rule.eic-gate-result` v3 (a married answer is $0). Unmarried gets the
single amounts (537, 236); married keeps v98's $0; unanswered blocks line
27a and the bottom lines and shows the question. Every other filer, and every
head of household with a counted child, keeps every value. All 775 recorded
lists are single and pass the strict full-surface replay with only declared
structural changes. **Recommendation: publish variant m as core v99, registry
v97, release v97.**

## 1. Trace: how v98 decides the credit by filing status

`rule.eic-status-class` v1 (`packages/content/tax/2025/rule.eic-status-class.json`)
publishes `tax.us.2025.eic.status-class` from filing status alone: 0 for
married filing separately or head of household (`:25-62`), 2 for married
filing jointly (`:64-81`), 1 for single or qualifying surviving spouse
(`:83-121`); its notes say so (`:139`). Seven v98 rules read the class:

| Rule (v98) | Reads | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `rule.eic-early-stop.v2.json:68-69` | column count = 0 and class = 0 | early stop: line 27a is $0 (`rule.eic-eligibility.v2.json`, `rule.form1040-line27a.json`) |
| `rule.eic-questions-required.v2.json:139` | column count > 0 or class != 0 | the remaining credit questions are required only then |
| `rule.eic-answer.separated-spouse.json:90-114` | asked when questions required, column count > 0, class = 0; no publishes 0 (`:53-56`) | Step 3 questions 3-5 |
| `rule.eic-answer.not-claimable-as-dependent.v2.json:114` | asked only for class 1 with no child | Step 4 question 6 |
| `rule.eic-answer.not-qualifying-child-of-another.v2.json:97` | skipped for class 2 | Step 3 question 6 / Step 4 question 5 |
| `rule.eic-answer.spouse-valid-ssn.json:48` | asked only for class 2 | Step 1 question 2 (spouse) |
| `rule.eic-answer.no-unsupported-situations.v2.json:100` | column count > 0 or class != 0 | Step 2 / Worksheet B situations |

The column count is the counted qualifying children with a valid SSN, capped
at 3 (`rule.eic-column-count.json`). The table column and phase-out amounts
read filing status directly, joint or other (`rule.eic-table-key.json`,
`rule.eic-column.completed.v2.json`, `rule.eic-column.phaseout-threshold.v2.json`),
so a head of household already uses the "other" column a single filer uses.
`rule.eic-gate-result.v2.json` gives 0 at the first answer that publishes 0
(the separated-spouse answer at `:77`), else Worksheet A line 6.

**What each status gets:**

| Status | No counted child | With a counted child |

2025 Publication 501 from the hoh-qualifying-person P0; the 2025 Publication

| Document | sha256 (PDF) |
| --- | --- |
| 2025 Instructions for Form 1040 (i1040gi) | `482e9c487c608f1bbeaceef35bc3c0933e8b35443cfff447e4279d590468364a` |
| 2025 Publication 501 | `288b62a4917f3bc3df0bade7a22c23a8ae648ba9eb5086df5b8b50e881148ff6` |
| 2025 Publication 596 | `29e8c6695912318253743210fcd68aaa10ad874c76edcab4aa7dc4b23ff51dbf` |

**When a head of household can take the credit with no qualifying child.**
Step 4, "Filers Without a Qualifying Child", question 1: "Are you a married
taxpayer whose filing status is married filing separately or head of
household? ... Yes. STOP You can't take the credit." (i1040gi 4517-4527). A
head of household who is not a married taxpayer continues to questions 2-6
(age 25 to 64, main home in the United States, not a qualifying child of
another, not claimable as a dependent; 4531-4592) exactly as a single filer
does, and uses the same "other" table column. Publication 596 agrees: a filer
without a qualifying child must meet Rules 11-14 (chapter 3, 1673-1681, none
about filing status) and the chapter 1 rules, of which Rule 3 says "If you are
married, you must usually file a joint return to claim the EIC. However, there
is a special rule for separated spouses", and that rule requires a
qualifying child (531-555).

**How "married" is judged for Step 4 question 1.** The instructions do not
define it for the credit. Publication 596's EIC Eligibility Checklist
question 3 ("Are you filing a joint return with your spouse or do you meet the
special rule for separated spouses?") says "Answer 'Yes' if you weren't
married at the end of 2025" (2397-2398): marital status at December 31.
Publication 501, Marital Status (649-723): unmarried for the whole year if on
the last day of the year you are unmarried or legally separated under a
divorce or separate maintenance decree (divorce final by year end; an
annulment counts as never married); considered married if married and living
together, in a recognized common-law marriage, married and living apart but
not legally separated under a decree, or separated under an interlocutory
decree; a spouse who died during the year leaves the survivor considered
married for the year for filing-status purposes.

**How that relates to "considered unmarried" for head of household.** The
head of household test is "unmarried or considered unmarried" on December 31
(i1040gi 1126-1150; Pub. 501 1111-1130): a married filer is considered
unmarried for head of household if they file separately, paid over half the
cost of the home, the spouse did not live there in the last 6 months, and
the home was the main home of their child, stepchild or foster child for
more than half the year (i1040gi 1229-1250). That filer is still married for
the credit: Step 4 question 1 names head of household among a married
taxpayer's filing statuses, which is possible only for a considered-unmarried
filer, and Publication 596 speaks of filers who are "married, but qualify to
file as head of household or married filing separately under special rules
for married taxpayers living apart (see Rule 3)" (454-456). The same words open
Step 3 question 3 for a filer with a qualifying child (i1040gi 4474-4512); there
the separated-spouse rule (4818-4827; Pub. 596 542-555) lets a married head of
household take the credit, and v98 asks it.

So:

| Head of household who ... | No qualifying child | With a qualifying child |
| --- | --- | --- |
| was unmarried at the end of 2025 (never married, divorce final, decree of divorce or separate maintenance) | credit as single (Step 4 questions 2-6) | credit (Step 3) |
| was married at the end of 2025 and is "considered unmarried" (lived apart, child in the home) | **no credit** (Step 4 question 1) | credit if the separated-spouse rule is met |

**What the record proves.** Nothing on v98 separates the two rows:
`tax.us.2025.hoh.tests-met` yes means "unmarried or considered unmarried"
(its question names both); the separated-spouse answer is asked only with a
counted child and its yes covers both "not married" and "married but lived
apart"; a counted child takes the filer to Step 3, not Step 4; the spouse
boxes and the lived-apart question are read only on joint or separate
returns. A refusal inferred from the record would refuse unmarried filers; a
credit inferred from it would pay married ones.

**Bounds.** No accepted ADR names the earned income credit's filing-status
rules (ADR index digests). The earned-income-credit milestone's contract
recorded "Married filing separately and head of household without a qualifying
child are $0" (`earned-income-credit.md` Contract 1; its P0
`earned-income-credit-evidence/p0-law-and-scope.md`, Step 4 table and the
"Married filing separately" section, read Step 4 question 1 as stopping every
head of household), and `rule.eic-status-class` v1's notes encode it. This
milestone changes that clause (Decision 1). No rule note claims the
head of household case was tested against an unmarried filer. Whether the
Internal Revenue Code reads differently from the instructions was not
examined; the instructions and Publication 596 are the sources followed.

## 3. Design

**Recommended (variant m).** Content only (`design.py`):

- Vocabulary bundle `tax.us.2025.eic-marital-status.vocabulary` v1
  (`eic-marital-status.bundle.json`) with one return-level yes/no fact,
  `tax.us.2025.eic.not-married`, titled with the test, its sources and what
  no means.
- Input question `input-question.eic-not-married` v1:

  > You file as head of household and no child is counted as your qualifying
  > child for the earned income credit. Were you unmarried at the end of 2025?
  > Answer yes if, on December 31, 2025, you had never married, your divorce
  > was final, or you were legally separated from your spouse under a decree
  > of divorce or separate maintenance. Answer no if you were still married
  > then, even if you lived apart from your spouse and file as head of
  > household because you are considered unmarried: a married taxpayer filing
  > as head of household cannot take the earned income credit without a
  > qualifying child.

- `rule.eic-status-class` v1 to v2 (`rule-artifact.v14`, unchanged schema):
  married filing separately 0; head of household 1 when the column count is
  0, else 0; every other status v1's expression. It now also requires the
  column count, which every reader of the class already requires (directly or
  through `eic.questions-required`), so no new blocking reach. A head of
  household with a counted child stays class 0, so the separated-spouse
  answer is asked exactly as on v98.
- `rule.eic-answer.not-married` v1 (new, `rule-artifact.v14`, the shape of
  `rule.eic-answer.separated-spouse`): 1 when the remaining credit questions
  are not required, a child is counted, or the status is not head of
  household; otherwise 0 on a no, 1 on a yes, and blocked naming the answer
  when it is missing.
- `rule.eic-gate-result` v2 to v3: v2 with the new answer checked after the
  separated-spouse answer; 0 when it is 0.

Package changes: replace two members, add three members and two entrypoints
(the bundle and the question; `build-m.json`). No v98 member pins either
replaced rule by id and version, so nothing cascades. `generate_to_directory`
validated the package, registry and release. Rule files: 4.4 KB and 3.4 KB.

The question is asked only where it can change line 27a: class 1 makes
`eic.questions-required` evaluate as for a single filer, so a head of
household whose AGI or earned income reaches the no-child completed amount
(19,104) is stopped on that amount instead and asked nothing (hoh/parent/50000).

**What a filer sees** (`probe-v99-*.json`):

Unanswered blocks 11 rules (the new answer, gate, eligibility, line 27a,
lines 32-37 and the 27-31 total); each of those page lines shows the question
as its only sentence. Lines 1-24 are unaffected. The yes amounts are the
single filer's at the same wages (537, 236).

**Rejected shapes.**

- Inferring "unmarried" from the record (`hoh.tests-met`, the
  separated-spouse answer, a counted child): none separates the two cases
  (section 2).
- Reading the answer in `rule.eic-status-class` itself: the class is read
  before any income test, so an unanswered question would block line 27a and
  the refund on every head of household without a child, at any income.
- Asking every head of household (with or without a child): the child case is
  already decided by the separated-spouse answer; the charter limits the
  question to the no-child case.
- Folding the answer into the separated-spouse rule (a class 3): the class is
  compared to 0, 1 and 2 by seven rules; class 3 would also need a successor of
  `not-claimable-as-dependent` (asked only for class 1), and the
  separated-spouse symbol would carry a second meaning.
- Asking the Step 4 question before the other Step 4 questions (so a married
  filer is not asked age and main home): each answer rule is required
  independently on v98, as for every filer; ordering them needs more
  successors for no change in any number.

## 4. Replay

`replay.py` over `tools/recorded_replay.py` (snapshots, diff, strict
classification): the 775 recorded lists, published v98 against the scratch
v99, nine workers, two shards (186 s of replay each, plus about a minute each
to load the lists).

Two checks are added on top of the declared expectations, because the change
is structural on every computed return: (1) a list record that differs only by
the inserted new answer (the report's row order, the gate result's `children`
and `missing`) is compared as equal and counted, and the report's row order
may also move the status-class row (it now reads the column count, so it is
evaluated later) when every other row keeps its order; (2) a derived
finding's alias drops its input-structure digest when its symbol has one
finding on each core, because the gate result's new input edge re-keys every
alias downstream of it; the edges are still compared record by record. Any
other difference fails the list. Declared expectations (10): the three
version changes (package v98 to v99, status class v1 to v2, gate result v2 to
v3), the new rule's report row and disposition node, the gate result's new
pin, the new graph node and edge, the question sentence, and the status
class's new column-count pin on a head of household.

## 5. Recommendation

Publish variant m as core v99, registry v97, release v97: the bundle, the
question, `rule.eic-answer.not-married` v1, `rule.eic-status-class` v2 and
`rule.eic-gate-result` v3, content only. An unmarried head of household with
no qualifying child gets the single filer's credit; a married one keeps $0;
every other filer, and every head of household with a counted child or at an
income where the no-child credit is $0, keeps every value and is asked
nothing new. Track 1: pin the probe cases live on v99 (parent and oldchild at
both incomes, unanswered, yes and no; hoh/parent/50000 asked nothing; the
child cases and the single and separate controls unchanged), plus the
recorded-list replay as the neighbour check. No engine, schema, projection,
page or kernel change, and no new ADR.

## 6. Decisions for the foreman

1. **The earned-income-credit contract's head of household clause.** That
   milestone's Contract 1 and `rule.eic-status-class` v1 say head of household
   without a qualifying child is $0. This change narrows it to a married head
   of household. Recommended: accept, and say so in Track 1's notes.
2. **Decree of separate maintenance.** Publication 501 counts a filer legally
   separated under a decree of divorce or separate maintenance as unmarried for
   the whole year, and the question follows it (yes). The instructions' Step 3
   question 5 lists a decree of separate maintenance among a married
   taxpayer's separated-spouse paths, which could be read as treating such a
   filer as married. Recommended: follow Publication 501's marital status.
3. **Spouse died in 2025.** Not named in the question: v98's head of household
   question already tells that filer they cannot answer yes. Recommended:
   leave it.
4. **A qualifying child without a valid SSN** is not counted (column count 0),
   so v99 asks such a head of household the Step 4 question. The instructions
   route that filer through Step 3 (separated-spouse rule) to a self-only
   credit, which a married head of household can take. v98 gives that filer $0
   and v99 still gives a married one $0; an unmarried one now gets the
   self-only amount, as the instructions also allow. The same pre-existing
   gap applies to married filing separately. Recommended: park.
5. **The separated-spouse answer has no input question**, so a head of
   household or separate filer with a counted child who has not answered it
   sees line 27a blocked with no sentence (hoh/childnosep). Outside scope;
   recommended: park or charter a one-question fix.
6. **Replay normalisation.** The strict replay needed two added checks (the
   inserted row and the alias re-keying, section 4) because the change adds an
   input to a rule on every computed return. Recommended: Track 1 keeps them in
   its neighbour check rather than declaring broader expectations.
