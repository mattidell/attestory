## The separated-spouse question and its source

Source: 2025 Form 1040 instructions, line 27a, Step 3, questions 3 to 5
(quoted):

> 3. Are you a married taxpayer whose filing status is married filing
> separately or head of household? Yes. Continue / No. Skip questions 4 and 5;
> go to question 6.
> 4. Did you and your spouse have the same principal residence for the last 6
> months of 2025? Yes. Continue / No. Skip question 5; go to question 6.
> 5. Are you legally separated according to your state law under a written
> separation agreement or a decree of separate maintenance and you lived apart
> from your spouse at the end of 2025? Yes. Continue / No. STOP. You can't take
> the credit.

Question text (the fact's yes means the credit can go on):

> You file as head of household or married filing separately and a child is
> counted as your qualifying child for the earned income credit. Does the
> separated-spouse rule let you take the credit? Answer yes if you were not
> married at the end of 2025; or you were married and you and your spouse did
> not have the same principal residence for the last 6 months of 2025; or you
> were legally separated according to your state law under a written separation
> agreement or a decree of separate maintenance and you lived apart from your
> spouse at the end of 2025. Answer no if you were married, you and your spouse
> had the same principal residence for the last 6 months of 2025, and you were
> not legally separated and living apart as described: you cannot take the
> credit, so it is $0.
