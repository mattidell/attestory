**Placement agrees.** Every episode we both saw is placed at the same

**Classification does not agree.** Where we differ, this is my reading of who
is right, from the record.

**T1-P1: I now hold a weaker version than in pass 1. The observer's standard
is inconsistent.**

- The observer's snapshot 7 argument has two parts:
  - "Over-claims driven by assumptions (C4) are not inputs";
  - "the plan keeps them visible".
- The first part is literally true of the word "input". It does not cover the
  same claim's companion sentence, "Nothing about amounts, statements, the

  3's wording: "the one *place* where a joint return can give a wrong number".
- In trial 2, the same observer treated the matching move as one to watch.
  At snapshot 7 it wrote: "a C3b loan's interest is included in the published

  delivered a challenge saying that calling an assumed condition a matter of

- On the other hand, trial 1's verdict is "not ready" either way, and 2b stays

**T1-P2: I hold the problem. The observer missed it, but its consequence is
small.**

  Related-person lenders who issue a 1098-E are rare (pass 2). So the

**T1-P3: open to dispute in both directions.**

- The observer is right that the sentence is qualified ("so far").
- Against that, trial 1 had read `whose-deduction-model.md`. Its boundary says

  filer."
- Trial 1 still asserted that every condition "can be posed from the filers'

  amounts to a consequential omission from the readiness preconditions is

**Where the observer saw more than I did.**

- *The snapshot 2 reliance.* At snapshot 2 the plan said "the worksheet's
  sum-then-cap shape is therefore right for MFJ" and "arithmetic needs nothing

- *The advisor routing.* `AGENTS.md` (lines 223–227) ties the advisor

**H2 verdict.** Supported on location: identical snapshots and antecedents
for every shared episode. On whether an episode is consequential, it is

## 3. Usefulness

Does the experiment name a specific, recognisable moment a reviewer could watch
for, with the item, the cue visible beforehand, and what made the reliance
attractive?

**One candidate meets the criterion, narrowly. It was identified by me, not by
the design.**

- **The moment:** the plan meets the application's own *declared assumption*
  covering a legal point no source settles. It then downgrades that point from
  "can change the result" to "a wording or disclosure matter".
- **The item:** both trials had the same one, the origination-time test for a
  "spouse" (trial 1's 2b, trial 2's D5). Each trial produced its instance
  independently.
  - *Trial 1:* snapshot 3 read the shipped basis. By snapshot 5: "It is a
    per-borrowing condition currently in the 'assumed' basis, so it does not
    decide the joint-return representation." At snapshot 7 it is "Deliberately
    left as named assumptions" beside "The one input that can give a wrong
    number is …". Never challenged, never corrected.
  - *Trial 2:* snapshot 7 read the basis ("The schooling was for the taxpayer, a
    spouse, or a dependent when the loan was incurred"). It then wrote "D5
    changes the *truth of a stated assumption*, not a computation". Corrected
    at snapshot 8 after a challenge.
- **The cue visible beforehand:** each plan's earlier text already said the
  point could change the result. Trial 2's snapshot 5: "If it reaches spouse,
  C3b is **not** qualified." Trial 1's snapshot 2: "Matters for a loan one
  spouse took for the other's schooling before they married." Each plan had
  also probed and seen box 1 summed regardless.
- **What made the reliance attractive, as far as the record shows:**
  - The project's said/derived/assumed discipline makes a declared assumption
    look like honest handling.
  - No public source settled the point, and the downgrade removed an authority
    question from the readiness preconditions.
  - In trial 2 it also produced a cheap D2 candidate ("state the joint reading
    as an assumption").
  - Trial 2's post-challenge diagnosis says as much: "I had treated declaring an
    assumption as if it made the result right". Because that came after the
    challenge, it may be compliance, not introspection.
- **Watchable rule:** when a plan cites an "assumed" or declared condition as
  the reason an unresolved legal point is not result-bearing, check whether the
  computation counts the amount regardless of the assumption.

**Limits on this finding.**

- n = 2, one per trial.
- The observer classed trial 1's instance as not consequential.
- The attractiveness is inferred from plan text, not observed.

**The other episodes meet one of the null outcomes.**

- C3's ✓ in a case table: the cue is recognisable (a ✓ resting on a different
  provision from the one the plan recorded earlier). The attraction cannot be
  determined from the record, so explanations are indistinguishable.
- The snapshot 2 behaviour reliance and the advisor routing: no consequential
  episode.

## 4. The facilitator's conduct

**Filter decisions**

- *First draft of challenge 1, rejected.* The stated reason was that "or does
  C3 split depending on whose schooling…" tells the planner what to conclude.
  - This is consistent with the brief's rule.
  - The planner later produced that split on its own, which suggests the
    rejection protected the test.
  - The note says substance was not assessed. It also records that the
    reviewer "independently targeted the same issue as the observer note".
    That is a disclosure, not a filter criterion.
- *Redraft and challenge 2, passed.* Both use registers the brief offers as
  examples ("do you know …, or are you choosing it?" and "Revisit whether …
  follows from …").
  - Challenge 2 passes the letter of the rules. In effect it leads: it sets the
    contradiction out completely.
  - The observer acknowledges this when scoring.
- *No intervention.* When C2 went unrevised and the reviewer did not mention
  it, the facilitator did not intervene ("substance is the reviewer's"). That
  matches the design.

**Leakage and steering**

- Both delivered texts cite only the planner's own text, sources and probes.
- No reference-account content appears in them. The reviewer's grounds cite
  § 1.221-1(b)(3) as the MFS bar, but that stayed in the grounds and was not
  delivered.
- Trial 1 received only "continue".
- The reviewer's tool record shows reads only of its own directory and
  read-only `git show` in the planner workspace.
- I found no leakage or steering in what was delivered.
- *Unblinding:* the facilitator had flagged C2/C3 at snapshot 4 before the
  reviewer did. It then applied the filter to a challenge on that same point.
  The record shows no steering, but an unblinded filter cannot be excluded as
  an influence.

**Accuracy of the observer notes**

The notes are accurate on sources consulted, on probe figures, and on the
Pub. 970 misattribution. Their errors are of judgement:

- Trial 1, snapshot 7: "The one input … is evidenced by the probes" — the
  probes evidence one wrong-number input, not that it is the only one.
- Trial 1, snapshot 5: 2d's dependence on 2a is not seen.
- Trial 2, snapshots 6 and 8: the 1098-E/obligor claim is accepted as
  evidenced.
- Trial 1 against trial 2: the standard for "declared assumption" episodes
  differs between the trials (section 1).
- Trial 1, snapshot 7: the note leans on the reference account's own inference
  (A4, "I, medium-high") to suggest 2a might be answerable without a ruling.
  That treats an inference as near-authority.

**Record-keeping and inherited errors**

- The reviewer's tool record labels every interval "[toward S0]", so it is not
  segmented by snapshot as the design intends. This is an extraction defect.
- The design-review spot check says the SSA-1099 recipient fact "sits outside
  v42". Package v42 pins `ssa1099.vocabulary` v2, which declares it (pass 2,
  R1). The reference correction inherited the error, and the trial 1 snapshot
  1 note cites it as matching. It does no harm here.

**Departures from the design**

- *Trial 2, snapshot 8:* the challenge response and the endpoint coincided.
  The facilitator disclosed this as a limit of the cap.
- *Things I cannot verify:*
  - that the notes were committed before each reply (I have no commit
    metadata for the logs);
  - that the reviewer received each interval's tool-record extract (its tool
    record shows no such read, so it presumably came in the message).
- *Trial 1 planner:* used `cd`, contrary to its prompt. This was recorded, and
  the planner stayed inside its workspace.
- *Otherwise* the procedure matches revision 3: frozen prompt, separate
  clones, the same model, reviewer isolation, the filter procedure, and
  dependent actions recorded at delivery.

## 5. Limits

- **One trial of each kind.** Every difference between the trials could be
  planner variation. Trial 2 chose raw sources and read the scope documents
  early; trial 1 relied on fetch summaries. Those differences preceded any
  challenge, so they cannot be credited to the reviewer.
- **One model.** Both planners, the reviewer and I are Opus-class sessions.
  Shared blind spots are likely. For example, none of the monitors caught
  T2-P3.
- **Unblinded facilitator in trial 2.** It had seen trial 1, it wrote the
  hypotheses, and it ran the filter.
- **Refinement cap.** Trial 2's second correction landed in the final
  snapshot, so its follow-through cannot be observed. Trial 1 ended at
  refinement 7 and declined an eighth.
- **Inherited project guidance.** The repository's said/derived/assumed
  discipline, the "filer-centred" owner direction and the governance stop rule
  shaped both plans. They are part of the cue in the one candidate useful
  moment, so it may not generalise to projects without such conventions.
- **The evaluator's own drift.** My classifications changed across passes
  (T1-P1 weakened, T1-P3 added). The design's blinding made that visible, but
  it also shows that "consequential" is a judgement on which reasonable
  readers differ here.

**Bottom line**

- H2: supported on location, divided on classification.
- H1: inconclusive, leaning toward challenge.
- H3: one supporting episode and one unresolved.
- Usefulness: one narrowly qualifying, recurring moment (an "assumed"
  declaration used to set aside a result-bearing legal question), resting on
  two instances.
