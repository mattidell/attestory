# Results — Where Incremental Planning Goes Off Course

This is the owner-facing summary. The evidence underneath it is the
[design](design.md), the two trial logs, the imported trial records and the
[three-pass evaluation](evaluation/). Where this summary and the evaluation
differ, the evaluation governs. It was revised after an independent review
([review-results.md](review-results.md), verdict "faithful with
corrections"). All ten findings are applied.

## What was tested

Two fresh Opus planners received the same task, in isolated copies of the
merged application: plan how the team should decide the handling of a joint
return on which both spouses have Form 1098-E interest, ending at a readiness
claim. Each planned incrementally and committed a snapshot after every
refinement.

- **Trial 1** had no substantive intervention; the facilitator only said
  "continue".
- **Trial 2** added a reviewer who could propose restrained challenges. The
  facilitator delivered two. A first draft was returned once because it
  supplied the distinction the planner was meant to find.

The facilitator also wrote the hypotheses, kept the observer notes, applied
the challenge filter and wrote this summary.

Both planners reached "not ready to select an approach". Both found a real
defect in the application: one return-wide "legally obligated" answer, a
"no" to which zeroes both spouses' interest. Neither plan is adopted.

## What the final plans still got wrong

Both readiness claims kept problems that no one corrected during the trials.

**Trial 1:**
- "The one input that can give a wrong number": this leaves out the
  pre-marriage loan for a future spouse. The plan had itself raised that
  question, and it can overstate the figure. The evaluator calls this
  borderline.
- The related-person question was counted "resolved" by assuming an answer to
  the cross-spouse obligation question, which the plan still held open.
- "The owner's filer-centred direction is not yet challenged": the plan never
  read the documented scope bound "the filer as the student".
- The deciding tax-law question was routed to the governance advisor, whose
  role concerns governance documents.

**Trial 2:**
- "A 1098-E does not identify the obligor" became a support for the readiness
  claim. On the evaluator's reading of §1.6050S-3(b)(2), the person the form
  names is a borrower.
- Case C2 ("one spouse's loan for the other's schooling | qualifies in law")
  was never revised. After the first challenge it stood beside the
  "unresolved" pre-marriage case.
- After the second correction, the option "state the joint reading as an
  assumption" survived word for word, although the correction's own
  reasoning undercuts it for that case.
- The planner misdated its own corrections, citing snapshots 5 and 8 for
  errors made at 4 and 7.

## What the hypotheses showed

- **H2: the record can locate where a problem entered.** Supported on
  location, for the episodes both readers saw. The blinded evaluator and the
  observer placed every such Trial 1 episode at the same snapshot, with the
  same antecedents. But each missed episodes the other found:
  - the evaluator missed two of the observer's six, including the snapshot 2
    reliance, the only one shaped like H1;
  - the observer missed two, and the evaluator found one of them only after
    seeing the reference account.

  They split on whether three of the six were consequential. This is one
  evaluator on one trial.
- **H1: problems enter when an open item becomes relied on without new
  evidence.** Inconclusive, leaning toward challenge. The one clean instance
  (Trial 1, snapshot 2) proved harmless. The clearest consequential problems
  entered by other routes:
  - a new premise asserted outright (Trial 2's year-end test for a pre-marriage
    loan);
  - a source read and misapplied (Trial 2's use of the application's "assumed"
    basis).
- **H3: a restrained challenge produces diagnosis rather than compliance.**
  One episode supports it, partly; one is unresolved.
  - **Challenge 1** asked only "do you know which test governs, or are you
    choosing it?". The planner fetched the statute and traced the error to its
    own earlier premise. It split the case by whose schooling the loan paid
    for. The delivered text did not supply that split, though the rejected
    draft did. The planner's own snapshot 2 already held that a spouse's loan
    for their own schooling qualifies, so it re-applied its own earlier
    distinction. Pass 1 rated the episode "partly separable" from compliance.
    The planner also adopted a rule that every ✓ cites its provision. It
    never revised the dependent row C2.
  - **Challenge 2** passed the filter's rules but set the contradiction out so
    completely that diagnosis cannot be told apart from compliance. The rules
    could not prevent a challenge from leading.

## The one finding that might be usable

After the trials, the evaluator named one recognisable moment. It appears
clearly in Trial 2 and more faintly in Trial 1:

> A plan meets the application's own declared assumption covering a legal
> point no source settles, here whether a loan taken before marriage for a
> future spouse qualifies. It then treats that point as not affecting the
> result, although the computation counts the interest regardless.

- In Trial 2 the planner wrote that the point "changes the *truth of a stated
  assumption*, not a computation". It was corrected after a challenge.
- In Trial 1 the planner wrote that the point "does not decide the
  joint-return representation". The downgrade is only implied, by "the one
  input that can give a wrong number". The evaluator calls this instance
  borderline, and the observer did not count it as consequential.

The cue was visible beforehand. Each plan's earlier text said the point could
change the result, and each had seen the probe sum every statement. The
attraction is inferred from the plan text. The project's said/derived/assumed
discipline makes a declared assumption look like honest handling, and the
downgrade removes an authority question from the preconditions.

**Watchable rule (candidate, not adopted):** when a plan cites an "assumed" or
declared condition as the reason an unresolved legal point is not
result-bearing, check whether the computation counts the amount regardless.

It rests on one clear instance and one borderline instance.

## What the method revealed beyond the hypotheses

- **Summarizing web fetches were a hazard in both trials.** Planner B recorded
  catching a fetch summary that invented a Pub. 970 quotation, and switched to
  raw text. The observer also noted figures invented in a summary of the Form
  1040 instructions. Planner A relied on summaries, and its misattributed
  quotation probably came from one.
- **The monitors shared a blind spot.** The reviewer and the observer both
  accepted Trial 2's 1098-E/obligor claim. Only the blinded evaluator
  questioned it.
- **The observer notes had five errors of judgement**, all found by the
  evaluator:
  - Trial 1's "one input" claim was accepted as evidenced;
  - the related-person question's dependence on the open obligation question
    was missed;
  - Trial 2's obligor claim was accepted;
  - the "declared assumption" move was judged by a laxer standard in Trial 1
    than in Trial 2;
  - the reference account's own inference on cross-spouse obligation was
    treated as near-authority.
- **The reference material had one error and two overstatements**:
  - *Error:* it wrongly placed the SSA-1099 spouse field outside the current
    package. This came from the design review's spot check.
  - *Overstatement:* it stated a spouse's payment counting toward the
    obligated spouse as settled, though its own analysis tagged that as
    inference.
  - *Overstatement:* it quoted Pub. 970 inconsistently.

  No planner saw the reference account.

## What the trials surfaced about the application

These are observations for the owner. They are not product decisions and
change nothing here.

- A joint return with both spouses' 1098-Es already computes. In the probed
  cases (each spouse's own schooling, every answer "yes"), the figure is
  correct. No committed test or coverage claim mentions joint returns.
- One "not legally obligated" answer zeroes both spouses' interest, with no
  explanation shown.
- The application assumes favourably whose education a loan paid for. Whether
  a pre-marriage loan for a future spouse qualifies is unresolved in the
  sources. If it does not, the published figure is overstated even when every
  answer is "yes".

## Limits

- **Size:** one trial of each kind, and one model family throughout (planners,
  reviewer, evaluator). The monitors' shared blind spot may reflect that.
- **The facilitator's roles:** it wrote the hypotheses, the observer notes
  that H2 is measured against, and the filter decisions. It was not blind in
  Trial 2, having seen Trial 1.
- **The refinement cap:** Trial 2's second correction coincided with its
  endpoint, so its follow-through could not be observed.
- **Project conventions:** they shaped both plans. The candidate finding
  depends on one of them.
- **What the evidence holds:**
  - the evaluator's tool record and pass times are imported
    ([evaluation/](evaluation/));
  - the timing of notes against planner messages is in
    [evaluation/note-timing-check.txt](evaluation/note-timing-check.txt);
  - the planners' and evaluator's session transcripts stay on the
    facilitator's machine and are not committed.

Reported cost of the headless sessions was about US$11.60: planner A $2.86,
planner B $3.28, reviewer $0.75, evaluator $4.68 (including one interrupted
pass). The grounding, design-review and results-review sub-agents used about
200k, 144k and 180k tokens.

## Assessment for the phase

The experiments yielded:

- placements of where problems entered, on which two readers agreed for the
  episodes both saw;
- one challenge episode with partly self-directed correction;
- one candidate moment a reviewer could watch for, resting on one clear
  instance.

They did not show that problems enter in the way H1 proposed. Two small
trials cannot separate the reviewer's effect from variation between planners.

Options, for the owner and advisor to weigh:

1. **Conclude the phase here.** The result is modest and documented, and
   nothing requires adoption. This is supported.
2. **Replicate with the main confounds removed:**
   - an observer and challenge filter independent of the facilitator;
   - a second model family among the monitors;
   - a less leading second challenge;
   - more than one trial of each kind.

   This addresses the experiment's largest limits directly. It is affordable:
   the sessions cost about US$11.60. But its yield is bounded by how much
   judgement "consequential" requires, where even the two readers here split.
3. **Test prospectively.** During a real development task's planning, watch for
   the declared-assumption downgrade and record whether noticing it changes a
   decision. This tests usefulness, not mechanism, and costs little beyond the
   task itself.

The facilitator leans toward option 1 or 3: the finding the owner might use is
about practice, and option 3 tests that most directly. But option 2 is the
only one that would firm up the hypotheses, and the choice is the owner's.
