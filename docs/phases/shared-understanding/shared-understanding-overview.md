# Shared Understanding in Development

## Purpose

The application now has a bounded example of a person's account changing over
time: reported student-loan interest, ordinary answers, calculation, correction,
explanation and return after a restart. Its development repeatedly exposed a
gap between what one participant thought had been established and what another
could demonstrate. The owner carried questions and findings between agents,
often after a draft or implementation was already complete.

This phase asks how agents can build a shared understanding while doing the
work. A planner should be able to ask someone who has investigated the case;
that person should be able to notice a mistaken interpretation and intervene.
The owner should receive a short account of the purpose, stakes and remaining
choices, with the technical evidence available underneath it.

This resumes the owner's earlier intention to investigate direct communication,
initiative, critical thinking and language as a working model. `OWNER_MODEL.md`
supplies the current owner and product models. These phase documents propose
experiments; they do not impose a new communication contract or change that
document on the owner's behalf.

The central material is the participants' explicitly expressed working model:
what they think the problem is, how its concepts relate, what they intend to
do, and what could change that understanding. These accounts let another agent
or the owner recognize a misunderstanding and help the acting agent locate it
within its own plan. The acting agent can then revise its model and actions.

“Thinking out loud” means making those accounts available as work develops.
It does not require narrating every tool call or producing an exhaustive
reasoning transcript. A short statement can expose a consequential premise;
the participants can expand it when the premise needs examination.

## Begin with work that has a consumer

The opening milestone asks a returning planning agent to model how ordinary
statements and favorable assumptions contribute to one student-loan tax
conclusion, while other applicable conditions remain represented as read-only
responsibilities without being established. These distinctions affect what the
application claims to know and what later development will build upon. The
Student Loan Circumstance Association milestone supplies concrete material;
the current implementation supplies evidence about what was subsequently built.

A case expert checks that material adversarially, then becomes available for
questions and challenges. The actual foreman facilitates the experiment; it is
not the participant under observation. Plans begin with actions the development
team will take to answer the question, before selecting application changes.

Agreement, disagreement, message count and documentation volume are not success
measures. Useful observations include recognizing that a decision could redefine
a tax concept, finding a gap in one's own model before choosing a mechanism,
revising an investigation after a challenge, or preserving a mistaken premise
despite expert access. A conversation that changes nothing is evidence too.

Choose tasks for the consequences of misunderstanding them. A minor wording
change can test delivery, but it is weak evidence about recognizing decisions
that shape future modeling. Participants should say what depends on a proposed
choice and why their next investigation is proportionate to those stakes. Do
not force dialogue when a straightforward action already has adequate grounds.

## How the phase develops

Start with channels the environment already provides. Build a message board or
automatic trigger when an observed communication need makes its purpose and
evaluation clear. Peer messages and repository threads are candidates; no
transport or protocol is selected in advance.

Later experiments may examine proactive advice during a technical build, how
an explanation survives replacement of an agent, or how concise project models
carry lessons without replaying every retrospective. High-level language should
give participants something to reason from. Terms such as “stakes” and
“plausible stand-in” are useful when attached to a particular claim or consequence.

Solicit participant feedback during each milestone and revise the roadmap from
the observations and owner feedback. Retain useful abstractions with an example
that explains them. Avoid turning a local observation into a universal instruction
before trying it in another setting.

## Scope and evidence

The phase can explore correspondence mechanisms, planning and build habits,
plain-language models of project guidance, and transfer of lessons. Formal
independent review continues to check consequential output; conversation does
not establish correctness by agreement.

Synthetic product work supplies the first setting. An agent reading a page can
provide an independent interpretation, but that does not establish human
layperson comprehension. Owner visual review and independent human reader
assessment remain distinct open observations. No personal tax data or
real-workspace entry is needed for the opening milestone.

Economics remain a consideration, especially owner mediation and duplicate
investigation. The opening experiment does not introduce token targets, repair
quotas or communication scores. First examine the meaning conveyed and the
decisions it changes; evaluate costs when that answers a concrete question.

## Completion and redirection

Reassess after each milestone. A useful boundary would be direct correspondence
used in real work, a clear account of where it helped or failed, and a small
practice tried in a second setting without requiring the original expert's
entire context. Any new channel must demonstrate delivery and response behavior
at its actual boundary.

The phase may conclude with a modest practice or evidence that the proposed
approach adds little. It does not require a general agent platform, A2A
implementation, new role hierarchy or governance rewrite. Its roadmap is
deliberately provisional.
