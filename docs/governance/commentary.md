# Commentary — First Edition (Minimal)

*Versioned governance artifact, lowest normative authority. Interprets; never amends. Organized as per-article notes plus three standing essays. Expandable by accretion; entries are dated and never edited.*

---

**Essay I — The decomposition of "fact."** *(Extends the Ontology's introduction.)* Ordinary usage compresses question-kind, question, and answer into one word, and the compression is harmless in conversation because context disambiguates. A system cannot rely on context. The three-way split is not an invention but a restoration: the older language of fact-finding — questions of fact, findings of fact, the finder of fact — already keeps them apart, and no one in that tradition thinks reality changed when a finding is corrected. The one deliberate departure from the *legal* register: findings here are made by the user for their own working record, or through instruments they adopted; nothing adjudicative is implied. Where the law has no word at all — the determination produced by adopted machinery — this system declines to force one, and the reserved entry records the instrument framing without pretending it is settled theory.

**Essay II — Why derived findings need no signature.** The argument, preserved whole because it is the collection's best: a derived finding contains nothing but findings the user already made, transformed by rules the user already adopted, through machinery that purity guarantees added no meaning of its own. Authority is compositional. Requiring a signature per derived output would be theater — the user approving mechanical consequences of what they already committed to. The absence of per-output signatures is not a gap in the signature model; it is the signature model working. The two vouching forms — inputs signed one at a time, machinery adopted wholesale — meet in the Contract article; the adoption is bounded rather than blank because the machinery is legible and versioned: obtuse terms are permitted, hidden terms are not.

**Essay III — Wholeness.** Atomicity and Reachability are halves of one commitment: the workspace never misstates itself, and never hides itself. An incomplete workspace is valid; a lying or concealing one is not. The pairing once lived in a single drafted principle and survives here as doctrine because each half is frequently tested alone — interruption safety without access, or access without truth — and passes falsely. The test that exercises both: stop anything, anywhere; step off any path; what you see is true and everything is there.

**Notes by article** *(selected; absent notes mean the article self-explains)*

**Art. 2 (Proposal).** Rejection records exist to escape a bad pair: re-propose declined claims forever, or keep a suppression list — which is the foreclosed pending store under another name. Rejection suppresses the *claim* (fact, value, basis), not the fact; new evidence reopens the conversation. — Verbatim capture solves the nondeterminism problem: a regenerated proposal may differ from what was signed, so presentation integrity must rest on the record, never on re-running the proposer.

**Art. 3 (Judgment).** The elective/determinable nature is declared per fact type, not discovered per situation; whether discretion *currently* exists may itself be derivable (eligibility rules), but the kind of question is declared. The fuller epistemic taxonomy — reporting, constituting, taking a position — is deliberately commentary, not ontology: the machinery needs only rule-governed supersession varying by type; the philosophy explains *why* the rules vary. Elections bind until rules permit change not because the workspace choice is legally operative — it is not — but because a tool that lets you build on a choice the law forbids is setting you up. Fidelity, not force.

**Art. 4 (Adoption).** The spreadsheet is the governing analogy: results belong to the person who built the sheet and entered its values. Adoption is where that analogy becomes structural — every published finding traces through a run to an adoption act to a person. Declining to re-adopt is equally a recorded choice.

**Art. 5 (Containment).** The problem with a second store is not staleness; a perfectly synchronized copy violates the article the moment it becomes necessary. Authority, not freshness, is the claim.

**Art. 7 (Supersession).** Correction and succession are the two shapes: same question answered again, versus the questions themselves changing. Displacement is eager as a matter of *validity* — a conclusion never outlives its premises in current state — while replacement is lazy as a matter of *work*; the workspace mid-cascade is incomplete, never wrong. Displacement is a consequence of the record, not a write fan-out; E7.1 exists because implementers reliably read "eager" as "synchronous."

**Art. 9/10 (Canon, Declaration).** Stock and flow: Canon governs everything standing, Declaration governs the moment of vocabulary growth — maximum scrutiny at the door, because an undeclared noun admitted once is a private understanding forever. Most growth is not new nouns: the thousandth field schema is data entry, an instance of an existing family. What Declaration guards is the rare claim no existing family can express — and the friction is the point: "what is this thing?" answered before the thing exists, not archaeologically after. Tolerance at the boundary is a second, unwritten schema — which is why Canon forecloses it inside while the Ontology permits it at intake, where output is only proposals.

**Art. 11 (Legibility).** The engine-version pin in run records is diagnostic, not contractual: the user adopts rules, not runners, and the portability test is what makes that adoption meaningful. A configuration format no human can recover the rule from is sealed behavior in a data costume — legibility is a property of recoverability, not file extension.

**Art. 14 (Record).** Records exist because current state cannot testify about past process: nothing in today's findings remembers what yesterday's run couldn't reach. Evidence, never ingredient — with the deliberate scoping that pre-assertion processes may consult rejection records, since the rule protects where authority is made.

**Art. 16/17 (Grant, Context).** Two questions with different masters: authorization (whose answer is a grant) and attention (whose answer is context). Holding data is not license to consult it — totality is an architectural property, not an invitation. Consent theater is not required: a document offered for extraction is offered for extraction, without per-field ceremony; what is foreclosed is presumption, bundling, and hostage-taking.

**Art. 18 (Quarantine).** A place, not a practice: trusted-not-to-look is a procedure; no-path-to-look is a wall. Evidence inherits sensitivity at submission; records inherit it by description — a run record over a real return discloses the shape of a life. If a bug reproduces only on real data, the defect is in the synthetic tooling.

**Art. 19 (Filing).** The one boundary where workspace act and legal act coincide, and where the weight deliberately changes. Everything upstream is the user's working record — operative for the workspace, weightless to the law. Filing is the largest assertion in the system, not the last derivation. The stance/position relation remains reserved: positions in the law's sense exist on filed returns; what filing does to workspace stances — ratifies, restates, reflects — is open vocabulary.
